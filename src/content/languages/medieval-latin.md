The Latin of medieval Europe, from the close of antiquity to the end of this atlas's span: no one's mother tongue after the Romance vernaculars went their own ways, but the working language of the church, the chanceries, the schools, and the crusades, written by more people across more of Europe than classical Latin ever was. Its spelling and grammar held close enough to the ancient norms that the atlas's romanization needs no special conventions; what changed was the world the words had to name, and the vocabulary stretched accordingly.

This file follows the precedent of Byzantine Greek: it hosts names that arise or change in the medieval period, the coinages of a Latin that was naming crusader states, new kingdoms, and rediscovered geographies, while classical forms that simply continued in medieval use remain under Latin with their own attestation windows. The file begins where the medieval story adds something the classical entry does not tell, and its first residents are the names Latin invented for the lands across the sea.
