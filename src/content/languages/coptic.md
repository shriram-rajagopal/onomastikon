The final stage of the Egyptian language, written in an alphabet adapted from Greek with seven additional letters preserving Egyptian sounds that Greek lacked. Coptic emerged as a literary language with the spread of Christianity in Egypt during the third and fourth centuries CE and remained a living spoken language through the early Islamic period. It survives today as the liturgical language of the Coptic Orthodox Church.
