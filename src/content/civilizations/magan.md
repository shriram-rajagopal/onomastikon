Magan was the metal-land of the Bronze Age Gulf, the mountainous country of the Oman peninsula, and perhaps stretches of the facing Iranian coast, from which Mesopotamia drew its copper and its prized black diorite. Its people, the Umm an-Nar culture of the archaeologists, smelted copper in the wadis of the Hajar range and shipped it north through Dilmun to the cities of Sumer and Akkad. The name appears in the records of the Sargonic kings, who traded with Magan and once fought it: Naram-Sin of Akkad campaigned against Magan and took its lord Manium captive, the farthest southeastern reach of Mesopotamian arms. Gudea of Lagash had diorite brought from Magan for the statues of himself that still survive, and the Ur III merchants ran a regular Magan trade out of Ur, wool and textiles south, copper north. After the Old Babylonian period the direct trade withered and the name with it, and it survived only in learned and archaizing use; the Neo-Assyrian kings could still deploy Magan and Meluhha as grand old words for the edges of the world, by then meaning Egypt and Kush rather than any place a ship could actually reach.

Like Dilmun, Magan wrote nothing of its own, and the name is known only from its neighbors; unlike Dilmun's, its thread may have continued under the empires. The Sumerian and Akkadian forms, *Magan* and *Makkan*, are one name in two mouths, and their afterlife is a genuine philological puzzle treated across this page's entries: the Achaemenid satrapy of *Maka*, in roughly the same country, is widely but not universally taken as the same name resurfacing, and the cape *Máketa* that Nearchus sighted at the mouth of the Gulf may carry it into Greek. What is certain is that the land itself never lost its work or its trade, and that its later Arabic name, *ʿUmān*, echoed already in the *Omana* of the classical geographers, belongs to a different thread entirely. One documented gap remains open on this page: the Elamite chancellery form *Makkaš*, attested in the Persepolis Fortification tablets, awaits a sourceable sign-by-sign spelling before it can stand as its own entry.
