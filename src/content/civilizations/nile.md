summary: The river of Egypt, flowing roughly 6,650 kilometers from the East African Great Lakes to the Mediterranean. The Nile's annual flood defined Egyptian agriculture, religion, and self-identity; Egypt's own endonym *Kemet* ("the Black Land") refers to the dark silt the river deposited each year.

Ancient names for the Nile fall into two broad groups. Egyptian itself used several internal names, primarily *Ḥꜥpy* (the divinized inundation, often anglicized as Hapi) and *jtrw* or *jtrw-ꜥꜣ* ("the river" or "the great river"). The Greek *Neîlos* and Latin *Nīlus*, the source of the modern name, are of uncertain origin; the most common etymology derives them from Egyptian *nꜣ-jrw* ("the rivers"), though Semitic and other derivations have been proposed.
