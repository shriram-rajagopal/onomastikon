The Sea of Marmara is the small inland sea between the two famous straits, entered from the Aegean through the Hellespont and drained into the Black Sea through the Bosporus, with Byzantium seated at its northeastern corner. Whoever held its shores held the passage between two seas and two continents, which is why so much of this atlas's later history, Greek colonization, the rise of Constantinople, the Ottoman crossing into Europe, happened around this one small body of water; Prokonnesos, its largest island, quarried the white marble that faced the capital's churches and palaces.

Its two names look at the same water from different directions and different millennia. Greek *Propontís*, "the fore-sea," is a relational name, the sea defined as what comes before the Pontos on the northward voyage, an itinerary fossilized into a toponym; the Arab geographers went further in the same direction and did not name it separately at all, treating strait, sea, and strait as one *khalīj*, the channel of Constantinople, on the Bosporus page. The Turkish name that replaced Propontis inverts the logic: *Marmara* names the sea for what is in it, the marble island, so the water that had been defined by where it led became defined by what it held. Between the two names sits the sea's whole story; the anteroom of the Black Sea ended up named for the stone that built the city on its shore.
