Khurasan is the great northeast of the Iranian world: the lands from the central deserts to the Oxus, organized around the four quarter-cities of Merv, Nishapur, Herat, and Balkh. The Sasanians made it one of the four quarters of their empire; under the caliphate it was the province that made and unmade dynasties, launching the Abbasid revolution from Merv in 747, and from the ninth century, under Tahirids, Saffarids, and Samanids, it became the ground where the Persian language returned to court and literature after two centuries of Arabic.

The name is a Sasanian compass-bearing: Middle Persian *Xwarāsān*, "sunrise," the east quarter, named from the vantage of the imperial center in Mesopotamia. It thus makes a matched pair with this phase's other great direction-name, *al-Maghrib*, the sunset-land, and the pair are not symmetrical by accident; the caliphate inherited Persia's east and named its own west, so the whole breadth of the medieval Islamic world ran, toponymically, from one people's sunrise to another's sunset. Unlike most administrative coinages the name outlived every administration that used it, and *Khorasan* remains on the maps of three modern countries. A region named "east" by an empire facing it; the empire fell, the direction stayed.
