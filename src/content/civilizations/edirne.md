Edirne stands where the Tundzha and Arda meet the Maritsa in eastern Thrace, on the highway from central Europe to Constantinople. The emperor Hadrian refounded the older Thracian town, called Uskudama in the sparse earlier record, as Hadrianopolis around 125 CE, and the position on the military road made the city a recurring battlefield; the catastrophe of 378, when the Goths destroyed the emperor Valens and his army outside the walls, made "Adrianople" a byword for Roman disaster in Ammianus's pages. Murad I took the city around 1369, and as Edirne it served as the Ottoman capital in Europe, the seat from which Mehmed II marched on Constantinople in the spring of 1453.

The chain on this page is one name aging in public: *Hadrianoúpolis*, the emperor's city, clipped by Greek habit to Adrianople, then carried into Turkish as *Edirne*, two syllables and a half in which Hadrian is no longer audible. The trajectory mirrors Nishapur's on the eastern side of this phase, a ruler's dedication dissolving into a plain place-name, but with a sharper final scene: the city named for the emperor who walled the Roman world became the base from which the Roman world's last wall was taken. For eight decades the Ottoman court sat in Hadrian's city planning the end of Constantine's; the atlas's final campaign was launched from one emperor's name against another's, with neither name still remembering its emperor.
