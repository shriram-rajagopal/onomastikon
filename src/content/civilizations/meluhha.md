Meluhha was the farthest of the three lands down the Lower Sea, beyond Dilmun and beyond Magan, from which the ships of the late third millennium brought carnelian, exotic timbers, gold dust, lapis-work, ivory, and strange animals to the quays of Mesopotamia. Most scholarship identifies it with the Indus civilization, the cities of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro and their ports, whose seals, etched carnelian beads, and weights have been found in Mesopotamian contexts of exactly the period in which the texts speak of Meluhha; the identification is likely rather than proven, since no ancient text draws the map for us. Sargon of Akkad boasts that ships of Meluhha moored at Agade, Gudea of Lagash lists Meluhhan materials among what he gathered for his temple, and an Akkadian cylinder seal preserves the profession of one Shu-ilishu, "interpreter of Meluhha," proof that the far land spoke a tongue no Mesopotamian knew unaided. In Ur III documents a "Meluhha village" near Girsu records a settled community of Meluhhans in Sumer itself. With the Indus cities' decline the sea-trade faded, and the name survived as a learned antique: to the Neo-Assyrian kings *Meluhha* meant Kush, the far south of a different sea.

Whether "Meluhha" is what the Indus people called themselves or their land is unknowable while their script stays unread, but it is the one candidate we possess for an Indus name heard live, and this page therefore stands beside the India page rather than inside it: *Bhārata* and *Hindu* descend from the subcontinent's own later traditions, while *Meluhha* is the Bronze Age name as the trade heard it, a thread that snapped rather than descended. Its two entries, Sumerian and Akkadian, are a single name shared by the two languages of the trade's home ports. The name's afterlife has one more curiosity: scholars have speculated, no more than that, that it survives in Sanskrit *mleccha*, the word for a non-Aryan foreigner, which would make the proud trade-land's name end as a slur in the mouth of the country that may once have borne it.
