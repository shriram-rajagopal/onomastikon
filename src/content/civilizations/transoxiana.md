Transoxiana is the land between the two great Central Asian rivers, the Oxus and the Jaxartes, with Bukhara and Samarkand as its capitals: the ancient Sogdiana of this atlas's earlier pages, renamed by the Arab conquest that crossed the Oxus in the decades around 700. Under the Samanids of Bukhara it became one of the most brilliant provinces of the Islamic world, and it kept its centrality through the Turkic dynasties down to Timur, whose empire was ruled from Samarkand at this atlas's closing edge.

The page holds one name because the name is a whole point of view. *Mā warāʾ al-Nahr*, "what lies beyond the river," is not a toponym so much as a position report: the region as seen from the caliphate's side of the Oxus, everything past the crossing. The English *Transoxiana* is the same thought in Latin dress, coined by Europeans translating the Arabic perspective rather than the land itself. Both stand in deliberate contrast to the entry for Sogdiana, where the region bears its own ancient name in its own language and its neighbors'; this page is what the same land is called when the namer stands outside it, on the far bank, looking across. Sogdiana is the view from Samarkand; Mā warāʾ al-Nahr is the view from Baghdad, and for seven centuries the world used the view from Baghdad.
