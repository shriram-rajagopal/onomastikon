Merv is the great oasis of the Murghab river, where the water runs out into the Karakum desert: the natural capital of the lands between Persia proper and the Oxus. It enters the record in the oldest layers this atlas has, named at Behistun among Darius's provinces and in the Avesta's list of good lands; under the Seleucids it was refounded as an Antiochia, under the Sasanians it anchored the east, and under the caliphate it became the seat of Khurasan, the city where the Abbasid revolution was raised in 747 and where the caliph al-Maʾmūn held his court. The Mongols destroyed it in 1221 with a thoroughness that became proverbial, and though the site revived in patches, the great city never returned.

The page's names are one name, older than every empire that used it: Old Persian *Marguš*, Avestan *Mouru*, Greek *Margianē*, Sasanian and Arabic *Marw*, Persian *Marv*, a single stem passed down some two and a half millennia with no change of thread, one of the longest unbroken name-chains in the atlas. The Behistun inscription's Elamite and Akkadian versions carry the same name in their own spellings; their cuneiform orthographies are not yet sourced here, the page's documented gap, per the same standard as Parthia's. What the chain ends in is the quietest entry of all: the modern Turkmen town beside the ruins is Mary, the ancient name worn soft by time, still on the ground where Darius's scribes first fixed it.
