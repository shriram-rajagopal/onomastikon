Bactra was the capital of Bactria, set on the oasis where the Balkh river fans out of the Hindu Kush toward the Oxus, at the crossing of the roads from Iran to India and China. The Achaemenids governed their eastern satrapies from it; Alexander wintered there during the campaigns beyond the Oxus; it was the royal city of the Greek kingdom of Bactria and then of the Kushans, and Zoroastrian tradition made it the city of the prophet's patron king Vishtaspa, remembering it as *Baxl ī bāmīg*, "Balkh the radiant." Under Islam it earned the title "mother of cities" before the Mongols destroyed it; the modern town of Balkh keeps the name on a much-reduced site.

The city's names are treated here alongside the land's, on the Bactria page, because for most of antiquity the two were one name: Greek distinguished the city *Báktra* from the country *Baktrianḗ*, but the Iranian tradition named city and land together, from the Avestan land-list's *Bāxδī* to the Middle Persian *Baxl*. This page carries the specifically urban thread: the Greek city-form that Herodotus already uses, its Latin echo, and the Arabic *Balkh* that continues the Middle Persian name of the city into the medieval world and the present.
