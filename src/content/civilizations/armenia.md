Armenia is the country of the Armenian highland, the mountainous land around Lake Van and Mount Ararat where the upper Euphrates and Tigris rise. Its people are among the oldest continuously attested peoples of the Near East, yet they answer to two quite different names. To themselves they are *Hay*, their land *Haykʿ* and later *Hayastan*, a self-designation traced in legend to the patriarch Hayk; to everyone else they are Armenians, after a name that first appears, fully formed, in the Old Persian *Armina* of Darius's Behistun inscription around 520 BCE, where Armenia is listed among the rebellious provinces of the new empire.

That exonym had deep roots in the same highland it named. The Behistun text's Babylonian column renders *Armina* not by a new coinage but by *Uraštu*, the old Assyrian name of the kingdom of Urartu that had ruled the region until two centuries before, so that the Persian scribes bound the new name to the old land it inherited. From the Old Persian the name passed into Greek as *Armenía* and through Greek into Latin, becoming the standard western designation, while the native *Haykʿ* stayed within the Armenian world. The result is a nation that answers to one name at home and another everywhere abroad, having never called itself by the word the rest of the world has always used for it.
