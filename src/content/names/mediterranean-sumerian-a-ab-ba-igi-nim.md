*a-ab-ba igi-nim* is the Sumerian name of the Mediterranean, "the upper sea," built from *a-ab-ba*, "sea," and *igi-nim*, "upper," which carries the same *nim*, "high," that spells the name of Elam. From the cities of southern Mesopotamia the sea lay upstream and to the northwest, at the far end of the rivers, and it was paired with the *a-ab-ba sig*, the "lower sea" of the Persian Gulf. The two appear together in the royal-inscription formula by which a king claimed to rule the whole earth, from the upper sea to the lower; the Sargonic and Ur III rulers measured their dominion by reaching both.

This is the headwater of the entire "Upper Sea" tradition: the Akkadian *tâmtu elītu* is a direct calque of it, and through Akkadian the upper-lower framing became the standard Mesopotamian way of bracketing the world, carried down to the Assyrian kings who recorded washing their weapons in the far water. The vantage is what the name preserves. The Egyptians knew this sea as the great green at their doorstep and the Greeks as their own Great Sea; the Sumerians, four hundred miles up the rivers, knew it only as the upper limit of the reachable world. The nearest sea to three later civilizations was, to the oldest of them, the farthest.
