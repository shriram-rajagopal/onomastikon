The Middle Persian name *Pūdīg* comes from the *Bundahišn*'s chapter on the seas, the same cosmological inventory that yields the *Kamrōd* recorded for the Caspian: of the three principal salt seas beside the world-ocean *Frāxkard*, the Pūdīg is the largest, the one in which there is flow and ebb, and the one joined directly to the wide-formed ocean. A tidal sea adjoining the open ocean on the empire's southern flank can only be the gulf on Iran's own coast, and the identification with the Persian Gulf has been standard since West's edition; the name is the tradition's own, with no ethnonym in it and no certain etymology. The attestation is Book Pahlavi, which has no Unicode encoding, so the entry carries transliteration only.

Every other name on this page points at Persia: the Greek *Persikòs kólpos*, the Latin *Sinus Persicus*, the Arabic *Baḥr Fāris*, all naming the water for the power on its northern shore, and even the Old Persian entry calls it the sea "that goes from Persia." The Persians' own tradition is the single one that does not. The Zoroastrian books name the gulf not for any nation but for its behavior in the order of creation, the sea that breathes. The world named the water after the Persians; the Persians named it after the tide, and between those two habits lies the whole difference between looking at a coast from outside and living on it.
