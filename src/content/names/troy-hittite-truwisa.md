A place-name in the Hittite records, *Taruiša* (also read *Taruiša*), written ta-ru-i-ša, that some scholars connect with the Greek *Troía*. It appears alongside *Wiluša* in a Hittite text listing lands of western Anatolia, which is part of what makes the equation attractive: if *Wiluša* is Ilios, a neighbor named *Taruiša* could be Troia. The identification is far less secure than that of Wilusa, however, and the reading and the connection are both debated.

*Taruiša* is the more fragile of the page's two Bronze Age threads, included because the symmetry it would complete is so striking: that Homer's *two* names for the city, *Ílios* and *Troía*, might *both* survive from the Hittite archives, as *Wiluša* and *Taruiša*. The first equation is firm enough to anchor the Trojan question; the second is a tantalizing possibility, offered here with the caution it deserves. Together they suggest that the doubled name of Troy is not a poet's invention but an inheritance, two ancient names for one citadel carried down from an age before Greek was written.
