The Byzantine theme of *Makedonía*, created around the 790s, the same military-administrative reorganization that produced the Anatolikon and the theme of Hellas elsewhere in this atlas. The name is the ancient one unchanged; the place is not. The theme's territory lay in western Thrace, with its capital at Adrianople, while the old Macedonian heartland around Thessalonike was administered under other themes entirely; for centuries of Byzantine administration, "Macedonia" officially meant a stretch of Thrace, and the dynasty called Macedonian, founded by Basil I, took its name from a founder born in the theme, near Adrianople rather than anywhere Alexander would have recognized.

The entry is the page's demonstration that an administrative name can drift clean off its geography and carry its history with it. The Old Persian entry above shows the reverse case, *Skudra* covering Macedonia from outside under a Scythian label; here the label is native, ancient, and correct in every respect except location. The "Macedonian" emperors of Byzantium's greatest age were Macedonian in exactly the sense the theme system allowed, which is to say Thracian; a name with a thousand years of geography behind it had become, in the empire's own ledgers, a bureaucratic coordinate. This page keeps finding names that are not where their places are; here, for four centuries, the place was not even where its name was.
