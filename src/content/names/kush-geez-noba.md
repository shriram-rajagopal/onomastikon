*Nobā* is the name Ezana's great inscription (RIE 189) gives the people against whom he marched to the Nile. The Noba, the text says, had crossed the Takkaze and attacked the peoples under Aksum's protection, the Mangurto and Hasa and Barya, and had boasted that the Aksumites would not dare to cross the river; Ezana crossed it, fought at the ford of Kemalke, and took their towns of masonry and of straw along the Nile, the region where Meroë had stood. Two centuries later the inscription of Kaleb (RIE 191, c. 525) still counts the Noba among the king's concerns. The spelling writes the second syllable in the fourth order, *bā*, and the first in the seventh, *no*.

The entry is the Geʽez cognate of Eratosthenes' *Noûbai*, six centuries later and on the other side of the river, and the agreement of the two is the best evidence that the name was the people's own rather than an Egyptian word for gold. Its place on the page is as the name of the inheritors. The Noba whom Ezana fought lived where the Kushites had, and the Christian kingdoms that followed Meroë, Nobatia first among them, would carry the name forward as Nubia. Ezana recorded, without knowing it, the handover: the stone that names the last of Kush names the first of Nubia in the same lines.
