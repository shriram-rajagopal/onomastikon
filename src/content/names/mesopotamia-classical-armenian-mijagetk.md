The Classical Armenian name for Mesopotamia, and not a borrowing but a translation: *miǰ-* "between" with *get* "river" and the plural *-kʿ*, the Greek *Mesopotamía* rebuilt piece by piece from native Armenian material. The form belongs to the fifth-century translation literature that created written Armenian, whose first monument was the Bible rendered out of Greek and Syriac, and it remained the standard geographic term thereafter.

Every other language on this page either coined its own image of the land or borrowed the Greek word whole; Armenian alone took the Greek idea and rebuilt it in its own morphemes, so that an Armenian reader needs no etymology to see the two rivers in the name. A loanword carries a foreign sound into a language, but a calque carries a foreign way of seeing, and this one shows the fifth-century translators working with such care that they translated even the map. The page's Greek entry names the land between the rivers; the Armenian entry is the same thought, thought again in Armenian. Armenian calls Mesopotamia *Miǰagetkʿ* still: the fifth-century calque has simply never been replaced.
