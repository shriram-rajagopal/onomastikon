The Persian name that the region bears to this day: *Panjāb*, *panj* "five" and *āb* "water," a precise calque of the Sanskrit *Pañcanada* made by the Persian-speaking world that took the region in hand from the Ghaznavids onward. Its earliest standard attestation is in Ibn Baṭṭūṭa, who crossed the region in the 1330s and records the name of the five waters; whether the traveler took it down from local Persian usage or the chancery's, the compound was in circulation under the Delhi Sultanate, and it settled from the rivers onto the land they define.

The entry closes the arithmetic this page counts through. The Vedic hymns numbered seven rivers, classical Sanskrit five, and Persian translated the five exactly, number and noun, into its own morphemes; like Armenian *Miǰagetkʿ* for Mesopotamia, it is a name carried across languages as an idea rather than a sound. Every stage of the region's name is a tally of its rivers taken by whoever governed them, and the Persian count is the one that stuck: the modern states of India and Pakistan both keep a Punjab, still counting five.
