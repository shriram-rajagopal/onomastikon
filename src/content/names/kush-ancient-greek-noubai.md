*Noûbai* is the earliest form of the name Nubia, and it enters the record as the name of Kush's neighbors rather than of Kush. Eratosthenes, writing around 230 BCE and preserved in Strabo, describes the Nile above Egypt: on the left of the river's course, in Libya, dwell the Noûbai, a great nation, reaching from Meroë to the bends of the river, not subject to the Ethiopians but divided into several kingdoms of their own. The old derivation of the name from Egyptian *nbw*, "gold," which would make Nubia the gold-land, is now generally set aside as a learned guess; the name is more likely the people's own, the same that Ezana's inscriptions write as *Nobā* six centuries later and that the Christian kingdom of Nobatia would carry into the Middle Ages.

The entry records a succession in advance. The Noûbai of Eratosthenes live beside the Meroitic kingdom, explicitly outside its rule; by the fourth century CE the Noba are in Meroë's place, and after them the name Nubia stands on the map where Kush had been. The Greek geographer who first wrote the name was describing a frontier people; he was also, unawares, recording the kingdom's successor. Nubia first appears in the record as the people next door to Kush, and ends up as the name of the house.
