*Khurāsān* (خراسان) is the Arabic form of the Sasanian quarter-name, taken over intact with the conquest of the 650s and kept as the name of the caliphate's greatest eastern province. In Arabic history the name carries enormous weight: it was from Khurāsān that Abū Muslim raised the black banners of the Abbasid revolution in 747, and "the men of Khurāsān" remained the byword for the dynasty's original army; Yāqūt's gazetteer treats the province at length, quarter-capitals and all, and reports the name's Persian meaning. The name remains in living use, on the map of Iran as its northeastern provinces.

Within the family the Arabic entry marks the hinge where a Persian administrative label became an Islamic historical one. Arabic did not translate the name into its own word for east, as it might have; it adopted the Persian word and then filled it with new content, until "Khurāsānī" meant a revolution, a style of piety, a school of scholars. The province named for the sunrise proceeded to behave like one: the Abbasid caliphate, Persian literature's rebirth, and the Seljuk Turks all rose out of Khurāsān and moved west across the empire. The caliphate took a name that meant "where the sun comes up" and found that dynasties came up there too.
