*Mada* is the Elamite form of the name, the middle column of the Achaemenid trilingual, written in the Mesopotamian cuneiform that Elamite scribes had adapted for their own language. It stands beside Old Persian *Māda* in the Behistun and Persepolis inscriptions, and the same name turns up in the administrative Elamite of the Persepolis Fortification Tablets, where Median officials and travelers on the royal roads are logged with their rations. The Elamite renders the Persian name closely, dropping only the long vowel its script could not easily mark.

Elamite is the chancellery language of the Persian heartland, the tongue of Susa and the bookkeeping of Persepolis, and its version of the name shows the Median province as it figured in the daily machinery of empire rather than the rhetoric of the monuments. Where the Old Persian *Māda* names a conquered kingdom in a king's boast, the Elamite *Mada* names a working administrative district, the same place seen from the ledger rather than the rock face.
