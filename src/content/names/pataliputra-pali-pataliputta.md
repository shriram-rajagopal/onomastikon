*Pāṭaliputta* is the Pali form of the city's name, the Middle Indo-Aryan continuation of Sanskrit *Pāṭaliputra* with the cluster *tr* assimilated to *tt*. Its great attestation is a founding scene: in the *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* the Buddha, on his last journey, passes through the village of Pāṭaligāma as Magadha's ministers Sunīdha and Vassakāra wall it against the Vajjis, and declares that so far as noble people travel and traders gather, this will be the chief city, *Pāṭaliputta*, though it will face ruin from fire, water, and discord. The canon thus names the metropolis before it exists, in the same breath as the village it replaced.

No other entry on this page catches a city at the moment of its naming. The Sanskrit form belongs to a capital already famous, the Greek to one at its imperial height, the Chinese to its old age among ruins; the Pali form belongs to a construction site, and its prophecy frame is the closest thing the family has to a birth certificate. The tradition Aśoka patronized remembered, or composed, the founding of his own capital in the mouth of the Buddha, and the vernacular doubling of *putta* is exactly how the emperor's own subjects would have said it.
