The Elamite rendering of Armenia in the Achaemenid inscriptions, *Harminuya*, written *har-mi-nu-ia*: the third voice of the trilingual chancellery, transcribing the Old Persian *Armina* with the prothetic *h*- that Elamite orthography favored before initial vowels. The form stands in the Elamite version of Bisitun through the long narrative of Darius's Armenian campaigns, five battles in a single rebel year, and in the land-lists of the later monuments.

Its interest on this page is the company it keeps, for the three chancelleries did not translate each other. Old Persian wrote the new Iranian name *Armina*; Elamite dutifully transcribed that Persian word; but the Babylonian column, as this page's Akkadian entry records, ignored the new name entirely and wrote the old *Uraštu*, Urartu, the dead kingdom whose land this was. The trilingual thus preserves a disagreement inside a single monument: two scripts calling the province by its future name, one by its past, carved side by side on the same rock. *Harminuya* is the middle witness, a Persian word in Elamite dress, marking the exact generation in which Armenia's name changed hands.
