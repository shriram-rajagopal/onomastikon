The early Islamic administrative name for Media, continuing the Middle Persian development *Māh* of the old *Māda* stem. The conquest-era sources divide the region into *Māh al-Kūfa*, "the Media of Kufa," centered on Dīnawar, and *Māh al-Baṣra*, "the Media of Basra," centered on Nihāwand, the two Māhs whose tax revenues were assigned to the two Iraqi garrison cities; al-Balādhurī records the arrangement in his conquest narratives. Umayyad dirhams struck in the region carry *Māh* as a mint name, giving the form documentary attestation in metal as well as text.

This is the last living link in the chain this page traces. Akkadian *Mādāya*, Old Persian *Māda*, Hebrew *Māday*, Greek *Mēdía*: every earlier entry names a people who still existed. By the seventh century the Medes were a memory a thousand years old, their kingdom folded into Persia by Cyrus before Rome was a republic, yet their name was still collecting taxes, stamped on coins, and filed in the caliphate's ledgers as a pair of fiscal districts. Media outlived the Medes as pure administrative geography, a name kept on the books because the books, not the people, were what endured.
