*Āl Saljūq* (آل سلجوق), "the House of Saljūq," is the Arabic name of the dynasty, standard in the historians from the eleventh century onward; Ibn al-Athīr's universal chronicle narrates their whole arc under it, from the first crossing of the Oxus to the fragments of the house ruling Anatolia. The eponym is Saljūq son of Duqāq, the Oghuz chieftain who led his people into Islam and the Jaxartes borderlands two generations before his grandsons Tughril and Chaghri took Khurasan; the Arabic spelling fixes a Turkish name whose own vowels are variously reconstructed, and every western form, Seljuk, Seldschuk, Selgiucide, descends from this Arabic orthography.

The entry names the atlas's clearest case of a grandfather outranking his empire. Saljūq himself never ruled a state, never crossed into Iran, and appears in history only as an ancestor; yet the sultans who prayed in Baghdad and the sultans who ruled Konya, a century and a half apart, both answered to his name, and modern historiography still files under it everything from the Oxus to the Aegean. The house had no country to be named for, having crossed into everyone else's, so the name of the man who started the migration became the name of everywhere it reached. Among the atlas's dynasty-names it is the purest: not a land, not a people, one dead chieftain, carried like a banner for three centuries.
