*Samarkand* is the Middle Persian name of the city as the Pahlavi geographical tradition records it. The *Šahrestānīhā ī Ērānšahr*, the Middle Persian catalogue of the cities of the empire, opens its survey of the sunrise quarter with the *šahrestān* of Samarkand and hangs a Kayanid foundation legend on it, crediting its building to the kings of the epic past rather than to any historical dynasty. The attestation is Book Pahlavi, which has no Unicode encoding, so the entry carries transliteration only.

The catalogue's opening is a quiet act of imagination, because Samarkand was never a secure Sasanian possession; the empire's real writ ran out near the Oxus, and the city belonged to the Sogdian princes whose own name for it is recorded on this page. The Pahlavi book of cities nonetheless begins Ērānšahr there, in a city beyond the border, assigned to the legendary Kayanids so that the geography of the empire could open at the eastern edge of the world it claimed rather than of the world it held. Every other tradition on this page names Samarkand as a real and reachable city; the Middle Persian entry alone names it as a wish.
