The name of Greece in the Babylonian Talmud, *Yāwān*, the biblical Ionian name carried into the rabbinic East unchanged in form and transformed in meaning. In the Bavli's usage *malkhut Yāwān*, "the kingdom of Yāwān," is above all a period and a power, the Hellenistic empire of the Seleucids against which the Hanukkah narrative is set, one of the four kingdoms of the apocalyptic scheme; the Greek language is *lāšon yəwānī*, and Greek wisdom, *ḥokhmat yəwānīt*, is a standing subject of rabbinic ambivalence, admired, restricted, and debated.

The entry is the Ionian family's rabbinic station, and its interest is how completely the referent moved while the word stood still. The Akkadian and Old Persian entries of this family named actual Ionians, traders and tributaries from the Aegean coast; by the Bavli's centuries *Yāwān* named no living state at all, for the Seleucids were six hundred years gone, but a permanent character in sacred history, the third empire, the archetype of alluring foreign wisdom. Every other language in the family eventually updated its referent, to Byzantines, to modern Greece; the Talmud kept *Yāwān* fixed at the moment of the Maccabees, and in the liturgy of Hanukkah it stands there still, a name preserved not by geography but by a holiday.
