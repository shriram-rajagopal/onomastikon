*Báidá* (白達) is the Song Chinese name for Baghdad, recorded in Zhao Rukuo's *Zhufanzhi* of about 1225, where it receives its own entry among the dependencies of the *Dàshí* realm as the residence of their supreme ruler. The characters, "white" and "reach," are sound only, a transcription of the Arabic name gathered from the sea merchants of Quanzhou whose reports Zhao, as superintendent of maritime trade, collected; his notice describes the caliph's city secondhand but names it accurately.

The entry joins the small Zhufanzhi shelf on this atlas, beside *Wùsīlí* for Egypt, *Ègēntuó* for Alexandria, *Sījiālǐyě* for Sicily, and *Májiā* for Mecca on this cluster's pages: the Islamic world's geography arriving in China by ship rather than by the old overland road. There is an unnoticed irony in the timing, for Zhao wrote within a generation of Baghdad's destruction; the *Zhufanzhi* preserves the caliph's capital at the far edge of the world's knowledge just before the Mongols, coming from China's own side of Asia, put an end to it. The name reached China westbound by sea a few decades ahead of the army that travelled the other way by land.
