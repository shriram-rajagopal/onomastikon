The Classical Armenian name for the Greeks and their land: *Yoynkʿ*, the plural of *yoyn*, in continuous use from the fifth-century beginnings of Armenian literature. The word is an Iranian loan, the same *Yauna* that the Achaemenid chancellery spread across its empire, taken into Armenian through the long centuries of Iranian linguistic influence that shaped so much of the classical vocabulary; the historians who wrote Armenia's wars and conversions in the fifth century already use it as the ordinary word for the Greek world they dealt with through Constantinople.

The entry extends this page's largest family into its tenth language, and it does so from an instructive direction. Armenia sat between the two empires whose names for the Greeks divide this page: Rome's heirs, who were Greek and did not say so, and Iran, whose *Yauna* the Armenians borrowed. Armenian Christianity looked west to the Greeks for scripture and councils, yet the everyday name for those same Greeks had come from the Persian side of the border. A church could take its creed from Constantinople while its language named Constantinople's people with a word out of Persepolis.
