The Arabic name for the Adriatic in al-Idrīsī's *Nuzhat al-mushtāq*, the great geography composed at the Norman court of Roger II in Palermo in 1154. Al-Idrīsī writes of the lands of the *Banādiqa*, the Venetians, and of the sea and channel that carry their name, treating the whole gulf between Italy and the Balkan shore as Venetian water; the usage reflects the twelfth-century reality in which Venetian convoys policed the sea from the lagoon to Otranto.

The name dates itself, and in doing so it completes a pattern the Greek entry above began. Antiquity called this sea after Adria, a single trading town at its head, and kept the name long after the Po's silt had stranded the town inland; the medieval Arabic name performs the same act of naming the whole sea for its dominant port, only with the successor. Adria's sea became Venice's sea, the harbor changed and the logic did not. Seas on this coast are named for whoever runs the trade, and the name is a dated receipt of who that was.
