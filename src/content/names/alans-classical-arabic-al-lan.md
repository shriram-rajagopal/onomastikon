The Arabic *al-Lān*, the geographers' name for the Alans of the Caucasus and their kingdom, the initial vowel of the old name absorbed into the article so neatly that the word looks native. The form belongs to the literature of the mountain frontier: al-Masʿūdī describes the king of *al-Lān*, his armored cavalry, his alliance-by-marriage with the neighboring Sarīr, and the fortress guarding the pass the Arabs called Bāb al-Lān, the Gate of the Alans, the Darial that a millennium earlier had admitted the raid Josephus recorded. For the Muslim geographers the Alans were no longer steppe wanderers but a settled Christian mountain kingdom, one of the fixed peoples of the Caucasus chapter.

The entry shows the family's name at its most domesticated, and its referent at the same stage: raiders in the Greek and Latin entries, in the Arabic they are a monarchy with a foreign policy, taken seriously as a counterweight to the Khazars whose page this atlas now holds. The pass-name is the durable part. *Bāb al-Lān* and its Persian predecessor Dar-i Alān outlived the kingdom, and the Darial Gorge on today's Georgian-Russian border still carries the Alans' name through it, a people become a doorway, which for the guardians of the Caucasus roads is perhaps the most honest memorial.
