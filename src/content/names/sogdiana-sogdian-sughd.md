*swγδ* is Sogdiana's name in Sogdian, the land's own word for itself at last: a consonantal skeleton, conventionally read *Suγδ*, spelled with the script's γ and δ for the fricatives that Old Persian could only approximate. The form appears across the Sogdian corpus, in the Ancient Letters of c. 313 CE and in the Mugh documents of the early eighth century, where the ruler Dēwāštīč styles himself in Sogdian "king of Sogd"; the spelling varies between *swγδ-* and *sγwδ-*, always with the δ. The adjective *swγδyk*, "Sogdian," names the language and the people from the same base.

Every other form on this page is an outsider's: the Achaemenid trilingual's *Suguda*, the Avestan land-list's *Suγδa*, the Greek *Sogdianē* that Alexander's historians made canonical. This entry is the word the Samarkand merchants themselves wrote in the addresses of their letters, and its fricatives are the test the exonyms are measured against; Old Persian *-gd-* is now read as an attempt at exactly the *-γδ-* this spelling preserves. The name that the whole family transmits is, on this page alone, written by the people who bore it.
