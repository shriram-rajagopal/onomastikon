*Armenía* is the Greek name of the country and *Arménioi* its people, taken over from the Achaemenid *Armina* and first appearing in Herodotus, who counts the Armenians among the satrapies of Darius and notes their kinship with the Phrygians. The fullest early Greek picture of the land comes from Xenophon, who marched the Ten Thousand across its snowbound plateau in 401 BCE and described its villages, its underground houses, and its barley-beer in the *Anabasis*. By the Hellenistic period *Armenía* was the standard Greek designation, applied to the kingdoms of Greater and Lesser Armenia on either side of the Euphrates.

The Greek form is the pivot through which the Persian exonym became the European one. The Greeks took the name from the Achaemenid administrative world they knew, rendered the Iranian *Armina* with the familiar place-name ending *-ía*, and passed it on to Latin essentially unchanged. The Armenians' own name for themselves, *Hay*, never entered this western stream; from Herodotus onward the Greeks called them by the name their Persian overlords used, and so, through Greek, has everyone else.
