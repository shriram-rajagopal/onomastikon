*Tōgarmāh*, the "house of Togarmah," is a northern nation of the Hebrew Bible, a grandson of Japheth through Gomer (Genesis 10:3) and, in Ezekiel, a far-northern people who trade horses, war-horses, and mules at the markets of Tyre and march in the army of Gog. The name is widely, though not certainly, identified with the Armenian highland: the Armenians themselves adopted the identification, naming Tʿorgom as the father of their patriarch Hayk and calling their land the "house of Tʿorgom." The toponym most often compared is the Hittite and Assyrian *Tegarama* of the upper Euphrates, in the right region but not obviously the same word.

The entry is marked disputed because the equation rests on later tradition and geographic plausibility rather than on any biblical statement that Togarmah is Armenia. What the case illustrates is how thoroughly the Armenians later wove the biblical genealogies into their own origin story, claiming descent from Togarmah just as they claimed the resting-place of the ark on their mountains. Whether or not the prophet meant Armenia, the Armenians made the name theirs, an exonym adopted not from a conqueror's chancellery but from the Table of Nations.
