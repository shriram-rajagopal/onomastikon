The Classical Armenian *Hepʿtʿałkʿ*, the name under which the fifth-century historian Łazar Pʿarpecʿi narrates the doom of the Sasanian king Pērōz. Armenia, a Sasanian dependency whose cavalry rode in the king's eastern wars, learned of the Hephthalites the hard way, and Łazar's account of the campaign of 484, the feigned retreat, the concealed trench, the king of kings dead with his army in the eastern desert, is among the fullest for the event in any language; the plural *Hepʿtʿałkʿ* wears the regular Armenian ethnonym ending, the same *-kʿ* that marks the *Honkʿ* and *Alankʿ* of this atlas's neighboring pages.

The Armenian name carries a moral charge the others lack, because Armenian history had its own account with Pērōz: he was the persecutor against whom Vardan's generation had risen at Avarayr, and when Łazar recounts the persecutor's annihilation by the *Hepʿtʿałkʿ*, the chronicle does not mourn. The entry is thus the family's specimen of a name recorded by a vassal watching its master fall, the White Huns entering literature as the instrument, in Armenian eyes, of a justice the Armenians could not administer themselves. Every other language on this page names the Hephthalites as a threat or a curiosity; Armenian names them almost as a deliverance.
