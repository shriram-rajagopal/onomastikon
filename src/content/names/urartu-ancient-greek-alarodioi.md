The *Alaródioi* are a people Herodotus lists twice, grouped with the Saspires in the eighteenth satrapy of Darius and again in the muster of Xerxes' army, equipped like the Colchians of the Caucasus. The name does not obviously resemble *Urarṭu*, but many scholars have connected the two, reading *Alarodioi* as a Greek rendering, via an Urartian or Caucasian intermediary, of the same highland name, and treating the Alarodians as the descendants of the Urartians surviving into the Achaemenid period under Greek eyes. The identification is old and attractive but far from secure.

The entry is marked disputed because the equation rests on a contested chain of sound-changes and on geographic placement rather than on any explicit ancient statement. If it holds, *Alaródioi* is the only Greek echo of Urartu under anything like its own regional name, the kingdom glimpsed not through the Assyrian *Urarṭu* but through a separate northern channel into the Greek ethnographic tradition. If it does not, it is a reminder that the Urartians left so faint a footprint in the classical record that even their possible appearances are arguable; the Greeks who named Armenia after the Persians' *Armina* may never have known the older kingdom at all.
