*Khurāsān* (خراسان) in Classical Persian is the Middle Persian name come home, the direct continuation of Sasanian *Xwarāsān* in the language's next stage, now written in Arabic letters. It is everywhere in the literature because the literature was born there: the Samanid and Ghaznavid courts that revived Persian poetry sat in Khurāsān and Transoxiana, and the *Shāhnāma* itself, composed at Tus in Khurāsān, uses the name for the east of the Iranian world throughout. Persian usage still knows the word's meaning; the poets could and did play on *khur*, the sun, in their own province's name.

The entry closes the family's circle in a way the Arabic entry cannot. For Arabic, *Khurāsān* was a borrowed label; for Persian it remained a transparent word, "sunrise-land," in the mouths of the people who lived there, so that the province is the rare case on this atlas of a population inhabiting a direction and knowing it. The literature that Khurāsān produced then carried the name west and made it a legend in Persian letters, the homeland of the language's own renaissance. Most regions of this atlas are named by outsiders looking in; Khurāsān was named by an empire looking east, and then filled by people who looked at their own horizon and found the name still true every morning.
