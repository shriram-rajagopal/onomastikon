The New Persian form of the name, *Īrān*, continuing Middle Persian *Ērān* directly into the language's classical stage. The *Shāhnāma* is built on it: Ferdowsi's whole epic is structured as the war of *Īrān* and *Tūrān*, and through fifty thousand couplets the name stands for the land, the people, and the moral order at once, at a time when no state of that name existed on any map.

That is the entry's peculiar work: it carried the name across the centuries when Iran was an idea without a government. Between the fall of the Sasanians and the modern state, *Īrān* lived in poetry and geography rather than on coins, preserved by Ferdowsi's readers the way a language preserves anything it cannot bear to lose. The page runs from the Avestan homeland-name to this form without a break, and this is the link that reaches the present: when a twentieth-century state needed a name for itself, it took the one the poets had kept, and *Īrān* went back on the map after twelve centuries in books.
