*Jambudīpa*, "the island of the rose-apple tree," is the Pali name of the southern continent of Buddhist cosmology, and in practice the name of India: when the canon says something happens in Jambudīpa, it happens in the subcontinent. The name belongs to a mapped cosmos, one of four great island-continents around Mount Meru, each named for a marker tree; but its earliest epigraphic appearance is thoroughly political. Aśoka's first Minor Rock Edict, from the mid-third century BCE, announces what the emperor has achieved *jambudīpasi*, "in Jambudīpa," using the cosmological word for the real empire he actually governed.

Every other name on this page comes from the river: the Indus family, *Hinduš* to *India*, names the land from its western edge, as outsiders met it. *Jambudīpa* is the reverse, a name from inside and above, locating India not against a neighbor but within a universe. That an emperor could use it in an administrative decree shows how naturally the cosmological and the political map coincided from within; the name means, in effect, "the world we live in," and for Aśoka that world was India.
