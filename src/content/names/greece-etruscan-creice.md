The Etruscan word for a Greek, *Creice*, attested where most Etruscan words are attested, on the funerary monuments: it appears as an ethnic turned personal name, a man called "the Greek," with a feminine *Creicesa* built on it in the family inscriptions. The word carries the same *graik*- root as Latin *Graecus*, reaching Etruria either through Latin or directly from the Greeks of the southern colonies, whose traders and craftsmen had been in the Etruscan cities since the orientalizing age; which road it took cannot now be told.

On a page dominated by the East's *Yawan* family and Greece's own *Hellas*, *Creice* is the western answer, and the only member of the *graikoi* family in a non-Indo-European mouth. Its attestation says as much as its etymology: Etruria knew Greeks not as a distant sea-people but as residents, husbands, and in-laws, common enough that "the Greek" could serve as a man's name on his urn, the ethnicity worn as an identity inside Etruscan families. The name that Aristotle remembered as the Hellenes' oldest is preserved in Etruria as somebody's grandfather.
