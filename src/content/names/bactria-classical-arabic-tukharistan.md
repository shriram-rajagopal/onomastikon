The Arabic name of the old Bactrian country, *Ṭukhāristān*, "the land of the Tokharians," the standard designation in the early Islamic geographers for the upper Oxus lands around Balkh. The name was inherited ready-made from the Iranian world: it preserves the ethnonym of the *Tochari*, the people this atlas meets on the Yuezhi page, who came off the steppe in the second century BCE and put an end to Greek Bactria, and whose name then settled over the country they had taken. By the time Arab armies crossed the Oxus, a Bactria that had been *Bāxδī* to the Avesta and *Baxtriš* to Darius had been the Tokharians' land for seven centuries.

The entry closes the page's long sequence with a change of ownership recorded in the name itself. Every earlier form on this page, Avestan, Persian, Greek, is a variation on one root that had held since the Bronze Age; *Ṭukhāristān* is the moment the sequence breaks, the country renamed for its conquerors the way Britain became England. The page thus ends by joining another page's family: Bactria's final ancient name is a Yuezhi ethnonym wearing a Persian suffix inside an Arabic gazetteer, three empires deep in borrowed clothing. The oldest continuously named land in the eastern Iranian world enters the Islamic archive under the name of the nomads who ended its first history.
