The Etruscan name of Rome is given here as *Ruma*, with a reconstruction mark, on the model this atlas already uses for Veii: the city-toponym itself is not directly attested, but its derivative is. The François Tomb at Vulci labels one of its painted figures *Cneve Tarchunies Rumach*, Gnaeus Tarquinius the Roman, and the ethnic *rumaχ*, "of Rome," presupposes the base *Ruma* just as the goddess's attested name anchors the reconstructed *Vei*; the gentilicial name *Rumate* points the same way. Whether the Etruscan form was borrowed from an Italic *Roma* or is itself the source of the Latin name is an old and open question, for Rome under its Etruscan kings was a city where the name could have run in either direction.

The entry restores to this page the one neighbor whose relationship to Rome was neither conquest nor correspondence but custody. Every other language here named Rome from outside its walls; Etruscan named it from inside them, in the generations when Tarquins from *Tarχna* ruled it and Etruscan was heard in its forum. That the surviving attestation is an Etruscan painting of a Roman Tarquin, made about when Rome expelled the Tarquins, is the page's neatest closed circle: the language that may have given Rome its name kept, as almost its only record of the city, the name of the dynasty Rome threw out.
