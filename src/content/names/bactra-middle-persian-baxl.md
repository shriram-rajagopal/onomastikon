*Baxl* is the Middle Persian name of the city, the ancient *Bāxδī*/*Bāxtri-* worn down by a millennium of sound change to a single heavy syllable. The Sasanian catalogue of provincial capitals, the *Šahrestānīhā ī Ērānšahr*, names *Baxl ī bāmīg*, "Baxl the radiant," as the place where the *šahrestān* of Nawāzag was founded, the epithet the Zoroastrian books give the city as a matter of course; in Sasanian usage the name is squarely the city's, the old ambiguity between town and land having resolved toward the town. The spelling here is the Inscriptional Pahlavi rendering used for Middle Persian in this atlas.

The form is the family's bridge into the living present: Arabic took *Baxl* over as *Balkh* with a flip of the liquid, and every later map descends from that. It is also a study in compression, five steps of the same name running *Bāxδī*, *Bāxtriš*, *Báktra*, *Baxl*, *Balkh*, in which only the initial consonant survives the whole journey untouched. The radiant epithet outlived the empire that coined it; "Balkh the radiant" was still the city's honorific when Arabic geographers called it the mother of cities.
