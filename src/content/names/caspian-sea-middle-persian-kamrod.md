The Middle Persian name *Kamrōd* comes from the *Bundahišn*, the Pahlavi compendium of Zoroastrian cosmology redacted in the ninth century, whose chapter on the seas inventories the waters of creation: the world-ocean *Frāxkard* at the edge of the earth, and beside it the principal salt seas, among them the *Pūdīg*, which alone has tides, and the *Kamrōd*, "that which they pass by, in the north, in Taparistān." A sea lying north of Tabaristan, the Iranian province on the Caspian's southern coast, can hardly be any other water, and the identification with the Caspian has been standard since West's edition; the name itself carries no ethnonym and no certain etymology, and it is that single locating clause that ties it to the map.

Every other name on this page was coined by geographers, naming the sea for whoever or whatever stood on its rim. *Kamrōd* alone comes from scripture: the sea appears not as a place observed but as an item in the inventory of creation, listed among cosmic waters in a book about how the world was made. Iranian speakers held the Caspian's southern shore for the whole of its recorded antiquity, and they are precisely the namers who did not call it after a shore-people; the outsiders named the sea for its inhabitants, while the inhabitants' own tradition placed it, without an eponym, in the order of the cosmos.
