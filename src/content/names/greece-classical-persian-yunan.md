The Classical Persian name for Greece, *Yūnān*, carrying Middle Persian *Yunān* into the new literary language. In the classical literature it is above all the country of the philosophers and of Alexander's teachers; the falāsifa tradition that translated Greek thought into the Islamic world kept *Yūnān* as the standing name for the Greece of Aristotle, a place more of the mind than of the map.

The entry is the Ionian family's longest single relay. The name left the Aegean as *Yauna* in the mouths of Achaemenid clerks, and this Persian stage handed it onward to the whole eastern half of the family's modern range: Arabic, Turkish, Urdu, Hindi, and their neighbors all say some shape of *Yūnān* for Greece because Persian said it first, and Persian says it still. Twenty-five centuries after Darius's scribes filed the Ionians by name, the successor of their word remains the ordinary name of Greece from Istanbul to Delhi, which makes this quiet dictionary form one of the most consequential entries in the family it belongs to.
