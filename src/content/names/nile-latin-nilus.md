Direct Latinization of Greek *Neîlos*, used throughout the Roman literary tradition for the Egyptian river. The river was personified in Roman iconography as a recumbent bearded male figure (most famously in the Vatican's *Nile* statue, c. 1st century CE), often surrounded by sixteen putti symbolizing the sixteen cubits of optimal flood-rise. The form *Nīlus* is the source of nearly all modern European names for the Nile, including English *Nile*, French *Nil*, and German *Nil*.
