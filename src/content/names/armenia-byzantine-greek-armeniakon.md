The Byzantine theme of the *Armeniakón*, one of the empire's original great military districts, attested from the 660s, its name an adjective formed on the Greek *Armenía* of the entry above: the command "facing Armenia," of the army once stationed against the Armenian frontier. The theme itself lay not in Armenia but across northeastern Anatolia and the Pontos, and it dissolved in the disasters after Manzikert, when the frontier it was named for ceased to exist.

On a page anchored by the exonym family of *Armina*, the entry shows the name doing something none of its siblings do: naming not the country but the direction of the country, an administrative label built from a neighbor's bearing rather than a people's home. Together with the Anatolikon, "the Eastern," and the theme of Macedonia that sat in Thrace, it belongs to the Byzantine habit of letting army names harden into geography; soldiers of the Army of Armenia settled into a province that then was, itself, the Armeniakon, Armenia as a compass-point on maps that Armenia itself never entered. Every other name on this page points at the highland; this one points from it, Armenia as seen over the shoulder of the empire next door.
