The Mycenaean Greek name of the city, *te-qa*, written in the Linear B syllabary on the tablets found in the Bronze Age palace of Thebes, the Kadmeion. Thebes is one of the few cities, with Knossos and Pylos, that kept its own archive, so its name survives in the oldest written Greek, the administration of the palace recording *te-qa* among its accounts before the citadel burned around 1200 BCE.

*te-qa* is a small entry with a precise philological payoff. The sign *qa* spells a *labiovelar*, a *kʷ/gʷ* sound that Mycenaean Greek still had and that later Greek lost, resolving it variously into *p*, *t*, or *b*; *te-qa* stands for *Thēgʷai*, and that *gʷ* is exactly the sound that classical Greek turned into the *b* of *Thêbai*. The tablet thus shows the name a stage earlier than any alphabetic Greek can, the Bronze Age form with its labiovelar intact, the deepest root of "Thebes" and a direct glimpse of a sound that vanished from Greek before Homer.
