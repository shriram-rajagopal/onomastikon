The Lycian word most commonly read as "Mede," attested in two inscriptions. At Tlos the form *medese* stands beside the name of Arttum̃para, a dynast who bore an Iranian name, and is taken as styling him a Mede; on the Xanthos pillar the instrumental *medezedi* pairs with *trm̃mil[ije]di* in a clause Oreshko renders "hunting on horseback in the Lycian and Median style." The stem would answer to the Iranian *Māda*, with the vowel fronted as Lycian regularly fronts an inherited *a*.

The reading is not settled, and the label above says so. The contexts are broken and the Xanthos stele yields its sense grudgingly; the identification rests on the fit of sound and situation rather than on a bilingual, and the underlying stem could in principle be a native word of similar shape. What tips the balance for those who accept it is the usage itself, because "Mede" here would mean Persian, exactly as it did in Greek, where fighting the Achaemenids was "medizing" long after the Medes had ceded the empire to Persis. If the Lycians did the same, then two neighboring peoples, one fighting Persia and one fighting for it, both reached for the older nation's name to describe the empire in front of them: the Medes' final conquest was vocabulary.
