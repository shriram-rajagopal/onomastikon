The Middle Persian name of Cappadocia, written *kpwtkyʾy* in the trilingual inscription of Šābuhr I at the Kaʿba-ye Zardušt, c. 262 CE, where the king records that he burned, ruined, and plundered the province of *Kappadukiyā* along with Syria and Cilicia in his third campaign against Rome; the priest Kirdēr's inscriptions of the following generation name it again among the western lands the Persian armies reached. The form is not the old satrapy name carried down within Persian: its doubled *pp* and its ending mark it as a fresh borrowing of the Greek *Kappadokía*, the name of a Roman province taken in the shape Rome's Greek-speaking east gave it.

The entry closes a loop that this page's Old Persian *Katpatuka* opens. Darius's chancellery coined the administrative name; Greek Hellenized it into *Kappadokía*; and when a Persian empire next had occasion to carve the province's name in stone, it took the Greek form back rather than reaching for its own ancestor. Five centuries of Hellenistic and Roman Anatolia lie between the two Persian spellings, and the second is the first refracted through the enemy's language. An empire can bequeath a name to the world and still, when it returns, have to borrow it home.
