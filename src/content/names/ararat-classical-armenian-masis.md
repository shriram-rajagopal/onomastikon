*Masis* is the Armenian name for the great peak the rest of the world calls Mount Ararat, attested from the earliest Armenian literature. The historian Movsēs Xorenacʿi tells of the mountain in his account of the national origins, and in the Armenian tradition *Masis* is the towering double-coned summit that dominates the plain of the Araxes, the heart of the Armenian homeland. The name is wholly distinct from *Ararat*: Armenians did not, until modern usage borrowed it back, call their mountain by the biblical word, and folk etymology instead derived *Masis* from a legendary king Amasia.

This entry is the local counterpoint to the biblical *Ararat* that names the same highland. Where the Hebrew *Araraṭ* is the Assyrian kingdom-name *Urarṭu* narrowed from a country onto a peak, *Masis* is the mountain's name in the mouths of the people who actually live beneath it, owing nothing to the Mesopotamian or biblical traditions. The two names for one mountain, the imported *Ararat* and the native *Masis*, sit beside each other much as Armenia's own *Haykʿ* sits beside the world's *Armenia*: the inside name and the outside name, rarely the same word.
