*Ỉkš* is the Demotic form of *Kꜣš*, the Egyptian name of Kush as the cursive script of the Late and Ptolemaic periods wrote it. The spelling has grown a vowel at the front, the prothetic *ỉ* that Late Egyptian and Demotic set before words beginning in a consonant cluster, and that single added letter is the hinge of the whole family: it is the vowel Coptic kept in *ečōš*, and it is the reason the Egyptian name can be followed in one unbroken line from the Middle Kingdom boundary stelae to the Christian monks of the Nile. The word names the land and, as an adjective, its people, in the texts of Egypt's southern frontier through the Persian, Ptolemaic, and Roman periods, down to the Demotic graffiti that Meroitic envoys to the temple of Isis at Philae left in the third century CE.

The entry has no glyphs to show, since Demotic has no usable Unicode encoding, and its reconstructed reading is anchored on its Coptic descendant rather than guessed. Its work on the page is structural: Demotic is where the name lost its hieroglyphic hills and gained the vowel that Coptic would keep. Without it the Coptic *ečōš* would look like a different word from the hieroglyphic *Kꜣš*; with it the two are the same name a thousand years apart, caught in the middle of changing.
