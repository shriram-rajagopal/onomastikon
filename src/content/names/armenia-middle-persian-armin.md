The Middle Persian name of Armenia, written *ʾlmny* in the great trilingual inscription of Šābuhr I at the Kaʿba-ye Zardušt, c. 262 CE, where *Armin* stands in the king's list of the lands of Ērānšahr and the king's own son Ohrmazd-Ardašir bears the title of great king of Armenia. The form continues Old Persian *Armina* directly, the same name in the same imperial register, and it remained the Sasanian administrative name through the marzpanate that governed eastern Armenia until the Arab conquests.

The entry closes a circle the page opened at Behistun. Darius carved *Armina* into his rock in the sixth century BCE; seven and a half centuries later a second Persian empire carved the same name into a different rock, twenty kilometers away, for the same country. Between the two inscriptions Armenia had been Achaemenid, Macedonian, Artaxiad, Roman, and Arsacid, and the Persian chancellery name had waited out all of it essentially unchanged. Empires filed Armenia under the same word for eight hundred years, which is perhaps the strongest claim to permanence a name in this atlas can make: not that a people kept it, but that rival bureaucracies could not improve on it.
