*Qūniya* (قونية) is the Arabic form of the city's name, current from the Seljuk conquest of the plateau and fixed in the geographers; Yāqūt, writing while the Konya sultanate still ruled, describes it as the greatest city of the lands of Rūm and the seat of its sultan. The form shows the familiar Arabic treatment of Greek names, the initial vowel absorbed and the back consonant taken as *qāf*, but with the accent of a name learned late and by ear, from the spoken Greek of twelfth-century Anatolia rather than from books; the Arabic of this entry, unusually on this atlas, met its Greek in person.

Under this name the city did its greatest cultural work. Seljuk Qūniya was a Persian-writing court in a Greek-speaking countryside ruled by Turks, and its most famous resident embodies the layering: the refugee scholar from Balkh who taught and died there is known to the world as Rūmī, "the Roman," a Persian poet surnamed for Byzantium because his city answered to the sultans of Rūm. The entry sits at the exact center of that knot; *Qūniya* is a Greek name in Arabic letters for the capital of a Turkish state that called itself Rome. Every layer of the medieval Anatolian world is present in the one word, and none of the layers is Arabic; Arabic simply wrote the whole arrangement down, as it did for half this atlas.
