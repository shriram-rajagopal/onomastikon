The Classical Persian name for India: *Hindūstān*, the inherited *Hind* of Middle Persian fitted with the Iranian suffix *-stān*, "land of," the same builder that makes every *-stan* on the modern map. Ferdowsi's *Shāhnāma* uses it freely around the year 1000, and with the Ghaznavid and Ghurid conquests the word crossed the mountains it described: for the Delhi Sultanate, whose chancery and coinage were Persian, *Hindūstān* was the official name of the very country being ruled.

That is the entry's distinction on a page of sixteen names: the others were what outsiders called India from a distance, but *Hindūstān* moved in. Persian became the language of Indian administration for seven centuries, and its name for India was absorbed by India's own languages, so that Hindi and Urdu speakers still call the country *Hindustān* today. The oldest thread on this page thus completes a full circle: the Sanskrit *Sindhu* went west into Persian mouths in the sixth century BCE, changed its shape, gathered a suffix, and came home two thousand years later as a name Indians now use for themselves.
