*Propontís* (Προποντίς) is the Greek name of the Sea of Marmara, a transparent compound: *pro-*, "before," on the name of the Pontos, the Black Sea. Herodotus, describing Darius's crossing into Europe, pauses to give its measurements, five hundred stades wide and fourteen hundred long, between the Bosporus and the Hellespont; the name is standard across the geographers and stays in Greek use through the whole Byzantine millennium, naming the water outside Constantinople's own sea walls. It is attested from the era when Greek colonists from Megara and Miletus ringed these shores with cities, Byzantion among them.

The name's interest is its grammar of direction. *Propontís* only makes sense sailing north: the fore-sea is "before" the Pontos for a ship coming up from the Aegean, so the name preserves the approach route of the Greek colonization it dates from, an itinerary hardened into a toponym. It thus completes a set with the atlas's two strait-pages, the Hellespont and the Bosporus, the doors on either side of this room; all three are named from the traveler's experience of passing through rather than from anything the places contain. Greek named this whole seascape the way a pilot describes it, and *Propontís* is the pilot's word for the stretch where one sea is behind you and the greater one not yet begun.
