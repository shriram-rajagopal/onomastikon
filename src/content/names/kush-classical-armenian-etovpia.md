*Etʿovpia* is the Armenian Bible's name for the land of Kush, and it tells at once which Bible the Armenians translated from. The version made after 405 followed the Greek, and so where the Hebrew says *Kûš* the Armenian says Ethiopia: Genesis 2:13 has the river Gihon circling "all the land of the Ethiopians," *Etʿovpacʿwocʿ*, and the prophets and Kings follow suit. The spelling is Greek *Aithiopía* in Armenian clothing, the Greek *th* rendered with the Armenian aspirate *tʿ*, the Greek *-io-* with the *-ov-* that Armenian used for that sequence. Only in the Table of Nations, where the Greek itself had kept the personal name *Chous* for Ham's son, does the Armenian write *Kʿuš*, and there with the *kʿ* that Armenian reserved for Greek *chi*: the eponym came through Greek too.

The entry fixes the Armenian Bible on the Greek side of the page's great divide, and it does so with a double witness. The land got the Greek description, Ethiopia; the ancestor got the Greek spelling of the Hebrew name, with a Greek aspirate where the Hebrew had a plain *k*. Nothing in Armenian comes from the Egyptian word directly or from the Syriac that lay a mountain range away. For the Armenians, as for the Latins, Kush was a country they knew only from scripture, and they knew it in the language scripture reached them in.
