The Avestan *Xiiaona-*, the hostile people of the Yašts' epic layer, whose king Arəǰat̰.aspa wars against Vištāspa, the royal patron of Zarathustra himself; scripture makes them the archetypal enemies of the faith at its founding moment. Who, if anyone, the historical Xiiaona were is beyond recovery, and their placement in this atlas at all rests on what became of the name rather than on any identification of the original bearers: the disputed label is carried by the whole chain, not merely a link. The script rendering is the letter-by-letter normalization from the transliteration that this atlas uses for Avestan.

The entry is here because it is the seed of the strangest transmission on the Huns' page: a name from the legendary wars of scripture, perhaps a thousand years older than Attila, that late antique Persia reapplied to the Chionite invaders of its northeast, whom Greco-Roman sources connect to the Hunnic world. If the connections hold, the family's oldest form predates its referent by a millennium, a name waiting in scripture for a people to arrive and claim it; if they fail, the word belongs to a vanished Bronze Age enemy and its presence here is the record of Persia's great creative misidentification. Either way the entry teaches the same lesson from the opposite direction as the rest of the family: elsewhere the Huns' name traveled to meet the writers, but in Iran the writers had the name first, and the Huns traveled into it.
