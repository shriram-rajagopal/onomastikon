*Mēdía* is the Greek name of Media, and *Mēdoi* its people, the form through which the Median kingdom entered classical historiography. Herodotus gives the Medes a full prehistory, the rise of Deioces and the dynasty that ended with Astyages and Cyrus, and treats them as the immediate predecessors of Persian power. So closely were the two identified in Greek eyes that the Greeks routinely called the Persian invaders *Mēdoi* and the wars against them *ta Mēdiká*; a Greek who collaborated with the enemy was said *mēdízein*, "to Medize," the Medes standing for the whole Iranian east.

The Greek form renders the same Iranian *Māda* as the Old Persian, with the long *a* heard and written as the Greek *ē*, a regular substitution in loans of the period. From *Mēdía* the name passed into Latin and so into every European language, while the Greek verb *mēdízein* preserved a political reality of the early fifth century, when "the Median side" meant a choice as much as a place. It is the clearest case on the page of a name outliving its referent: the Medes were already a Persian province when the Greeks made their name a byword for the eastern enemy.
