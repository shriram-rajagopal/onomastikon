The Old Iranian name of the river, *Vaxšu*, reconstructed rather than read from any surviving Avestan page: the extant Avesta does not preserve the word, and the form is recovered from the witnesses that carried it, the Greek *Ōxos* and Latin *Oxus* on one side, the Sanskrit *Vakṣu* of the Indian river-lists on the other, and the Wakhsh, the river's major eastern headstream, which bears the name in living speech to this day. The Avestan-script rendering here is a normalized construction from that reconstructed form, on the same letter-by-letter principle as the atlas's attested Avestan spellings.

*Vaxšu* remains the headwater of the river's family of names even as a reconstruction; every other form on this page descends from or answers to it. Iranian tradition treated the great rivers as divine, and the Indian *Vakṣu* stands among holy rivers, but for this river the oldest layer of the name must be inferred from its children rather than quoted from a text. There is a fitness in that: the river that fed everything around it survives, in name, only downstream of itself.
