*Babylōn* (ⲃⲁⲃⲩⲗⲱⲛ) is the Coptic name of the fortress town, taken over from the Greek and carried in Coptic documents, martyrologies, and episcopal lists through the Christian centuries of Egypt and far beyond the Arab conquest. The see of Babylon was among the bishoprics of the Egyptian church, and the fortress quarter, with churches built into and above the Roman walls, remained a center of Coptic life while first Fustat and then al-Qāhira rose beside it; in time the patriarchate itself settled in the area, making the old fortress the effective heart of Egyptian Christianity.

The entry's significance is survival in place. Every other name on this page belongs to a new city built beside the old ones; *Babylōn* alone stayed fixed to its ground and its community, shrinking from the name of a Roman garrison town to the name of a quarter, but never lapsing, while the metropolis around it changed names twice. The Coptic quarter of Old Cairo is still called Babylon in the church's usage today, its Hanging Church suspended over the Roman gate towers. Cairo buried the fortress but never the name: the Copts still worship in Babylon, a city inside a city, keeping a name from another river a thousand miles from where it was coined.
