The Elamite rendering of Gandhara in the Achaemenid inscriptions, *Gandara*, written *gan-da-ra*, standing in the satrapy lists beside Old Persian *Gadāra* and Babylonian *Gandari*. Like the Babylonian, the Elamite column preserves the nasal that Old Persian's spelling conventions leave unwritten; the two cuneiform chancelleries, hearing the name read aloud, recorded the *n* that the king's own script silently swallowed.

With this entry the page's Behistun trilingual is complete, and its testimony is unanimous on the one point Old Persian obscures: the name really was *Gandāra*, nasal and all, exactly as the Sanskrit at the head of the page has always said. It is a neat inversion of authority, for the two borrowed renderings outvote the imperial original, and the province's own Indian name is vindicated by scribes in Susa and Babylon who never saw it. In the trilingual system the subject languages were meant to echo the Persian; here they quietly correct it.
