*Ālánliáo* 阿蘭聊 appears in the *Hou Hanshu*'s chapter on the western regions in a single administrative sentence of enormous historical value: the state of Yǎncài, it reports, has changed its name to Ālánliáo. The transcription's first two syllables carry the name *Alan*, and the notice is the Chinese registry's record of the same transformation the western sources show from their side, the old Aorsi confederation of the Caspian steppe reorganizing, in the first centuries CE, under the Alanic name. The earlier form, *Yǎncài*, is treated on the Sarmatians' page, and the pair of entries records the rename across the two pages exactly as the Han clerks recorded it across two dynastic histories. Chinese IPA is omitted per this atlas's policy for reconstructed ancient readings.

The entry is the atlas's cleanest specimen of a name-change captured in a foreign archive in real time. Greek and Latin writers watched Sarmatians shade into Alans gradually and never marked the moment; the Chinese bureaucratic habit of registering states by current name forced the transition into a dated sentence. That the westernmost people of the Han world-registry can be checked against the easternmost people of Ptolemy's, and that the two accounts agree, is one of the quiet triumphs of ancient record-keeping, two empires bracketing the steppe and, without knowing it, keeping each other's books.
