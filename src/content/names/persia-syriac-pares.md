*Pāres* (ܦܪܣ) is the Syriac name of Persia, fixed in the Peshitta Old Testament by Daniel's "king of Media and *Pāres*" (Daniel 8:20) and the nobles "of *Pāres* and Media" of Esther 1:14. The gentilic *Pārsāyā*, plural *Pārsāyē*, names the Persians, as in the Peshitta's Ezekiel 27:10, with the adverb *pārsāʾīṯ*, "in Persian," for the language; the Church of the East called its ecclesiastical province in Fars *Bēth Parsāyē*, "the house of the Persians." The consonants are those of Imperial Aramaic *Prs*, already entered elsewhere on this page: the same Aramaic word continued rather than reborrowed, now vocalized by the Syriac tradition as *Pāres*.

What distinguishes the Syriac name is where it was written. The Church of the East lived inside the Sasanian empire, and Syriac names Persia overwhelmingly from within: in the acts of the Persian martyrs under Šāpur II, in the synodal records of Seleucia-Ctesiphon, in the documents of communities for whom the *Pārsāyē* were their own kings and neighbors. Just as the Elephantine papyri show Aramaic naming Egypt from inside Egypt, Syriac names Persia from inside Persia. Aramaic had been the chancellery language of the Achaemenids, and *prs* one of the words its clerks carried across their empire; seven centuries later it was the language of the Christian subjects of that empire's Iranian successors instead. Dynasties changed, and the standing of the language changed with them, but whoever ruled Iran, its Aramaic-speaking scribes went on writing the same three consonants.
