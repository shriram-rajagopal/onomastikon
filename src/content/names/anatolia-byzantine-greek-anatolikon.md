The name of the greatest of the Byzantine themes, the military province that covered the central plateau of Asia Minor from the later seventh century: *to Anatolikón*, "the Eastern," formed from *anatolē*, the sunrise. Constantine VII's *De Thematibus* opens his catalogue of the empire's provinces with it, explaining the name by the army it housed; its general was for centuries the most dangerous subject the emperors had.

The name preserves a retreat in amber. The army it named had been the field army of the East, *Anatolē* in the old sense, the command that faced Syria and Mesopotamia; when the Arab conquests took those lands, the army fell back into Asia Minor and kept its name, so that "the East" came to designate the plateau where the eastern army now stood rather than the east it had lost. The ancient entry above records *Anatolē* as the sunrise-lands seen from Greece; the theme fixed that name to the peninsula itself, and from this administrative accident the whole landmass is Anatolia today. A name for somewhere else, stranded by defeat, became the permanent address of the ground the defenders held.
