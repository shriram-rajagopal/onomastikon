*Mādāya* is the Akkadian name of the Medes, the gentilic "the Medes" used both for the people and their land. It is the oldest attested form of the name, predating the Achaemenids by nearly two centuries: the Neo-Assyrian annals of Shalmaneser III, Sargon II, and their successors record campaigns against the "mighty Medes" of the eastern mountains from the ninth century onward, long before Media was a unified kingdom. The same form later serves as the Babylonian column of the Achaemenid trilingual, rendering the Old Persian *Māda* in the classical Mesopotamian cuneiform.

The Akkadian form thus spans the whole arc of the name's documented life, from the Medes as a loose confederation of mountain chiefdoms harassed by Assyrian kings to Media as a settled satrapy of the Persian Empire. It is also the form closest to the Hebrew *Māday*, both Semitic languages taking the Iranian name through the same Mesopotamian channel; when the Assyrians first set it down, the Medes were still a scatter of mountain chiefdoms, not the empire their name would one day help to rule.
