The Latin name of the Merv country, *Margiana*, the Greek *Margianē* in Roman dress. Pliny gives the region its fullest Latin notice: a land of famous sunniness and vines, ringed by desert, where Alexander founded a city that the nomads destroyed and Antiochus rebuilt as Antiochia Margiana. Pliny also attaches to Margiana the most haunting rumor Rome possessed about the place, that the soldiers captured at Carrhae were marched east and settled there, the lost legionaries of Crassus ending their days as border guards of an oasis they had never heard of.

The entry joins the *margu* family at its westernmost and least firsthand point. The Avestan and Persian forms belong to scripture and administration, the Arabic to conquest and residence; the Latin form belongs to hearsay, a name Rome knew chiefly as the address of its own missing men. No Roman army ever reached Margiana, but in the Roman imagination Romans were already there, captives at the far end of the world's longest road. On a page of names given by rulers and neighbors, *Margiana* is the name given by the bereaved: the oasis as the last known location of the disappeared.
