*Margianḗ* (Μαργιανή) is the Greek name of the Merv oasis and its district, formed from the Old Persian province-name with the Greek territorial suffix; Strabo describes Margiane as a plain of extraordinary fertility ringed by desert, and Pliny repeats the theme, adding that Alexander had founded a city there which, destroyed, was refounded by Antiochus as Antiocheia of Margiane. The Hellenistic refoundation gave the city itself a dynastic Greek name for a time, but the district name, the old Persian one in Greek dress, is what the geographers actually used and what survived.

The entry catches the one moment in the family's long chain when the name was nearly replaced. Antiocheia Margiane follows the pattern this atlas meets at Nishapur and Alexandria, the founder-king stamping himself on a site; at Merv the stamp did not take, and the king's name needed the old name beside it even on its own coinage of usage, "of Margiane," to say where it was. When the Hellenistic layer wore off, *Margu-* was simply still there underneath, and every subsequent language took up the old stem as if the interruption had never happened. Kings could rename the city; nobody could rename the oasis, and in the desert it is the oasis that owns the name.
