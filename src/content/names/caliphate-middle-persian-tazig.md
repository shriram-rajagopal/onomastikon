*Tāzīg* is the Middle Persian word for an Arab, and after the conquest, for the Muslim Arabs and their realm. It is generally derived from the tribal name Ṭayyiʾ, the north Arabian tribe on the Sasanian frontier, extended with the Iranian adjectival suffix *-īg*: the Arabs nearest Persia lent their name to all Arabs everywhere, the same mechanism that made a corner tribe of Greeks into the *Graeci*. The word belongs to the Zoroastrian Pahlavi books, written in Book Pahlavi script, which has no Unicode encoding; the entry therefore carries transliteration only, per the same rule as Demotic.

This unassuming adjective is one of the great travellers of the atlas. It went east along the Silk Road to be transcribed as Chinese *Dàshí*, crossed the mountains into Armenian as *Tačik*, and continued in Persian itself as *Tāzī*, so that from the Tang court to the Caucasus the caliphate was known by a Sasanian word for a border tribe. Its later career is stranger still: in Central Asia the word drifted from meaning "Arab" to meaning "Muslim" to meaning the settled Persian-speakers among the Turks, and it survives today as the name of the Tajiks and of Tajikistan. A name that began as Persia's word for the Arabs ended as the world's word for a Persian-speaking people; no other form on this page has wandered so far from its first referent, or landed anywhere so unexpected.
