The Classical Armenian name for Persia: *Parskʿ*, formally a plural, "the Persians," used from the first generation of Armenian literature for both the people and their empire. The stem is the Iranian *Pārs* itself, taken directly from the neighbor it named, and dressed in the Armenian plural *-kʿ* that the language also uses for its own *Haykʿ*; the later literature builds the fuller *Parskastan* on the same base. The fifth-century historians use it constantly, for the empire whose wars, marriages, and persecutions fill their pages.

No language in this atlas wrote more about Persia from closer range. Greek observed Persia across a sea and Akkadian across a scribal tradition, but Armenian wrote about Persia from inside its political orbit, in books whose great subject is precisely the struggle to not become Persian; the word *Parskʿ* appears in Armenian literature most often with an army attached. The name is borrowed from the Persians' own *Pārsa*, unchanged at its core, which gives the entry its edge: the literature most devoted to resisting Persia never resisted the name, and fought the empire for a thousand years while calling it exactly what it called itself.
