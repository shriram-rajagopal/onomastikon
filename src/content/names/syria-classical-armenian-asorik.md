The Armenian name of Syria, *Asorikʿ*, the plural of *asori*, "Syrian," the same ethnonym Armenian still uses for its Syriac-Christian neighbors. The word came through Iranian, and inside it sits the name of Assur: *asori* continues the Assyrian root, so that Armenian, like Greek before it, names Syria with a word carved off Assyria. The fifth-century translators and historians use *Asorikʿ* for the Aramaic-speaking country to Armenia's south, the churches and schools of Edessa and Antioch with which Armenian Christianity was in constant conversation.

The page's Greek entry records the famous shrinkage by which *Assyría* lost a syllable and came to mean the western country; *Asorikʿ* is the same event witnessed from the opposite side of the mountains, through Iranian rather than Aegean ears. That two unrelated routes, a Greek clipping and an Iranian loan, converged on the same result is the strongest kind of evidence for what the Çineköy inscription finally confirmed: Syria really is Assyria's name gone west. Armenian adds the human detail the others lack, for *asori* remained a living word for actual neighbors, teachers, and fellow Christians; in Armenian the old imperial name did not fossilize into geography but kept walking around, attached to people.
