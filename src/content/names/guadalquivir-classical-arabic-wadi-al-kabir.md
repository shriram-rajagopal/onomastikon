*al-Wādī al-Kabīr* (الوادي الكبير), "the great river," is the Arabic name of the Baetis, in use from the conquest and standard in the geographers; al-Idrīsī's twelfth-century survey follows it past Qurṭuba and Išbīliya as the artery of al-Andalus. It is barely a name at all, just the common noun *wādī*, "river valley, watercourse," with the plainest possible adjective; Andalusi usage knew other phrases for stretches of it, but this was the one that stuck. The name remains the Arabic term for the river today.

The entry documents the atlas's most complete victory of description over denomination. Castilian did not translate the phrase or revive the ancient name; it swallowed the Arabic whole, and *Guadalquivir* is simply *wādī al-kabīr* in a Castilian mouth, as *Guadiana* and dozens of lesser Guad- rivers are its siblings across the peninsula. The result is a river whose modern name is a fragment of the conquerors' ordinary speech, fossilized by the people who expelled them: every Spaniard who names the river recites four words of Arabic without knowing it. Baetis was a name and it died; "the great river" was only a sentence, and sentences, it turns out, can be immortal.
