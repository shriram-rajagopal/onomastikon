The Babylonian rendering of Margiana in the Bisitun inscription, *Marguʾ*, written *mar-gu-ʾ* with the aleph sign closing the word where Old Persian writes the sibilant of *Marguš* and Elamite *Markuiš*. The land earns more than a list-entry at Bisitun: Darius's narrative records the revolt of Frāda the Margian in his first regnal year and its suppression by the satrap of Bactria, so the Babylonian column names *Marguʾ* not merely as a possession but as a battlefield, with a body-count that the Babylonian version alone inflates to tens of thousands.

The name belongs to the oasis's deepest layer, the *margu* family that runs from Avestan *Mouru* through Old Persian *Marguš* to Middle Persian *Marw* and Arabic *Marw*, and the Babylonian form is that family's one excursion into cuneiform Mesopotamia. Its final aleph is the sound of a scribe negotiating a foreign sibilant his own late dialect had gone soft on; the same lists that transcribe *Gandari* and *Katpatukka* letter by letter simply let the end of *Marguš* fall off. Merv's page runs on a six-form chain across two and a half millennia, and this, the shortest and most mangled link, is the only one written in the script of Babylon: the oasis of the far east, filed briefly, and imperfectly, in the oldest filing system in the world.
