The Arabic name for the Parthians is not a toponym or an ethnonym but a verdict: *Mulūk al-Ṭawāʾif*, "the kings of the factions," the standing designation in al-Ṭabarī and the Arabic-Persian historical tradition for the powers that held Iran between Alexander and Ardashīr. The frame was inherited from Sasanian historiography, which taught that after Alexander the land was parceled among petty regional kings, in disorder, until Ardashīr restored the single throne; the Arsacids, who in fact ruled for nearly five centuries and stopped Rome at the Euphrates, appear inside that frame as the largest of the squabbling parties.

The name is the revenge of the successors written into the record. The Sasanians, who destroyed the Arsacids, also curated their memory, and the version Arabic inherited compressed Parthia's half-millennium into an interregnum between two legitimate empires; the dynasty's own name, the *Parθava* this page traces through five languages, survives in the Arabic tradition mostly in scattered forms, while the standing label stayed a dismissal. Arabic found the phrase so serviceable that it used it again, centuries later, for the taifa kings of a fragmented al-Andalus. Every other language on this page names Parthia as a place or a people; the Arabic names it as an accusation, and the accusation was written by the empire that buried it.
