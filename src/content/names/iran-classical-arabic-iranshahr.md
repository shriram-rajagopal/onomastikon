The Arabic geographers' name for the Iranian land, taken directly from Middle Persian *Ērānšahr*, the Sasanian state-name. Writers of the ninth and tenth centuries, Ibn Khurradādhbih, al-Yaʿqūbī, and al-Masʿūdī among them, organized their descriptions of the world on the Iranian *kishvar* scheme of seven climes and began with *Īrānshahr* as the central one; Yāqūt's thirteenth-century *Muʿjam al-Buldān* still carries the name as a headword, glossing it as the whole country of the Persians.

What is remarkable is who kept the name alive. The Sasanian state fell to the Arab conquests in the mid seventh century, yet its official self-designation survived for another six centuries inside the geographic literature of the conquerors, not as an antiquarian curiosity but as a working term for the region's place in the ordered world. On this page, *Ērān* names the idea from within and *Ariane* reports it from without; *Īrānshahr* is the rarer thing, an endonym adopted whole by the empire that ended the state that coined it, and the bridge over which the old name walked into the modern one.
