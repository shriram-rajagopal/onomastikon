*Balkh* is the Arabic name of the city, the Middle Persian *Baxl* taken into Arabic with the liquid and fricative exchanged, a metathesis of the kind border names often undergo. From the Arab conquest of Khurasan in the later seventh century the name is constant in Arabic historiography and geography; Yāqūt's great geographical dictionary gives the city a long entry under *Balkh* and transmits its proudest title, *Umm al-bilād*, "mother of cities." The form is a continuity case at this atlas's edge, carried past Late Antiquity because it is the living end of the ancient name.

On this page *Balkh* is the terminus: the only form still in daily use, the name of the modern Afghan town and province that sit where *Bāxδī* was praised in the Avesta. The family thus runs unbroken from the oldest stratum of Iranian religious poetry to a present-day address, some three thousand years on one oasis, and the Arabic entry is the link that got it across the medieval divide. Few names in the atlas connect their first attestation to a working post office by so short a chain.
