The Armenian name of Rome, *Hṙom*, worn smooth by an Iranian mouth before Armenian ever took it: the initial *hṙ-* is the giveaway, the same Middle Persian *Hrōm* that this atlas records on Rome's own page, since Iranian could not begin a word with *r-* and prefixed the breath that Armenian then faithfully kept. Armenia, church and court alike, learned the name of Rome not from Romans but from the Iranian world it lived inside, and the form runs from the fifth-century historians through the whole of Armenian literature; modern Armenian still says *Hṙom*.

The trilled, breathed *Hṙom* is a small phonetic fossil of Armenia's position on the map. Greek and Latin sit on one side of the page, Iranian on the other, and the Armenian form shows which neighbor stood between: even the name of the West arrived from the East. Its sibling on the Byzantines' page, *Hoṙom*, is the same loan taken again for the same empire's Greek continuation, so Armenian ended up holding the Roman name twice, once for the old city and once for the new; both times, the word had to pass through Persia to reach a country that was, for most of its history, closer to Rome's enemies than to Rome.
