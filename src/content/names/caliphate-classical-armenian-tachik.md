*Tačik* (Տաճիկ) is the Classical Armenian name for the Arabs, used in the plural, *Tačikkʿ*, for the Muslim power that replaced Sasanian Persia on Armenia's southern horizon. It appears in the bishop Sebeos, whose history, written around 661, is the earliest surviving substantial account of the rise of Islam in any language, and it remains the standard Armenian word for the Muslim neighbor through the medieval period. The word is an early borrowing of Middle Persian *Tāzīg*, taken over with the affricate that marks the older stratum of Iranian loans in Armenian; Armenia learned its name for the Arabs, like so much of its political vocabulary, from Persian.

The entry is the exact sibling of *Hoṙom* on the Byzantines' page, and the pair say something about where Armenia stood. Both of Armenia's imperial neighbors, the Christian empire it shared a faith with and the caliphate it answered to, are named in words learned from a third power that had ruled it before either. And like *Tāzīg* everywhere it travelled, the word refused to stay put: as Muslim rule over Armenia passed from Arabs to Turks, *Tačik* drifted with it, until in later Armenian it meant the Turk next door. The word tracked not a people but a relationship, whoever currently ruled the Muslim world as seen from Armenia. Armenia's names for its neighbors are a small archive of its history: conquered in Persian, they name Christians and Muslims alike in the conqueror's old words.
