*Aithiopía* on this page is the Greek name of Kush in its second life. In the Greek versions of Ezana's inscriptions the king's title runs "king of the Aksumites and of the Himyarites and of Raeidan and of the Ethiopians and of the Sabaeans," and the Ethiopians in that list are not the people of the Nile but the king's own highland subjects, the Ḥabašat of the Geʽez version at the same place. The usage was established by the fourth century: the Greek inscription of an earlier king at Adulis, copied by Cosmas Indicopleustes, used the Ethiopian name for the conquered peoples of the highland and the coast, and the Greek-speaking world of the Red Sea, which had called the whole African shore beyond Egypt Ethiopia since Herodotus, let the Aksumite kings claim the word.

The entry is the hinge between the Kush page and this one. The Greek description that Homer and Herodotus had meant for the Nile became, in a translator's choice on Ezana's stones, the Greek name of the kingdom across the Red Sea, and because the Greek Bible used the same word for Kush, the choice carried scripture with it. From here the name goes into Geʽez as *Ityoṗya* and into the kingdom's sense of itself. On the Kush page *Aithiopía* is a name the Greeks gave; on this page it is a name a king took, and the taking is the moment the modern map was made.
