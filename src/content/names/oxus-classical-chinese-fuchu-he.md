The Chinese name of the Oxus, 縛芻 *Fúchú* with the generic *hé*, "river," set down by the pilgrim Xuanzang in the *Da Tang Xiyu Ji*, his great report on the western countries compiled on his return to Chang'an in 646. Crossing and recrossing the river as he worked through the kingdoms of Tokharistan, Xuanzang transcribed the name he heard on its banks, the Indian and Buddhist *Vakṣu*; the characters are pure phonetics, chosen for sound, in the same transcription practice that gives this atlas his *Xìndù* for the Indus and the *Héng hé* for the Ganges.

The entry extends the *vaxshu* family to its easternmost witness, and it certifies the family's claim in a way no western form can. The Greek *Ōxos* and the Arabic *Jayḥūn* reached their languages through middlemen; Xuanzang stood on the riverbank and wrote down what the people beside the water called it, a millennium after the Avesta's world and a decade before the Arab conquests would rename the river's whole basin. His three characters are the last record of the river's old Iranian-Indian name taken from life, by the one namer in the family who verified it in person.
