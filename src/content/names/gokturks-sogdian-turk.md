*twrk* is the Sogdian spelling of the Turks' name, conventionally read *Turk*, with a plural *tr'wkt*, *Türkit*, and its earliest carrier is the qaghanate's own first monument: the Bugut stele of the 580s, raised for Taspar Qaghan, is written not in Turkic but in Sogdian, the language of the merchants who ran the empire's chancellery and its diplomacy. The form recurs across the Sogdian corpus wherever the steppe's masters are named, and the rendering here uses the Old Sogdian letters this atlas employs for the language.

The entry documents the strangest fact in the whole family: the first empire of the Turks first wrote its own name in someone else's language. For its opening decades the qaghanate spoke Turkic and wrote Sogdian, so the name *Türk* entered the written record at home and abroad through Sogdian intermediaries, the same merchants who carried it to Constantinople in 568. The plural *tr'wkt*, with its Sogdian ending, is very likely the shape that reached the Chinese court as *Tūjué*, making this small trading language the distribution point for the name in both directions. On this page the Sogdian form sits among the exonyms, but for a generation it was as close to an official spelling as the empire possessed.
