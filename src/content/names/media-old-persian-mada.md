*Māda* is the Old Persian name of Media, written in the alphabetic cuneiform Darius commissioned for his monuments and attested across the Achaemenid royal corpus. It appears in the Behistun inscription among the lands Darius claims and the provinces that rose against him, and recurs in the lists of subject peoples at Persepolis, Naqš-e Rustam, and Susa. The Medes themselves left no written form of the name; *Māda* is the Persian rendering of a self-designation in the closely related Median language, which survives only as loanwords and names embedded in Old Persian.

This is the headwater of the name as the wider world received it. Because the Medes wrote nothing of their own that survives, the Achaemenid trilingual is the earliest place the name is fixed in script, and the Old Persian column is its monumental face: the Elamite and Babylonian versions stand beside it, and the Greek *Mēdía* that gives Europe the word is a parallel rendering of the same Iranian original. The name's stability across so many scripts begins here, in the Achaemenid trilingual, the earliest writing to fix in script a name the Medes themselves never recorded.
