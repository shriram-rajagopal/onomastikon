The Arabic name of Crete, *Iqrīṭish*, the Greek *Krḗtē* taken into Arabic with a prosthetic vowel to open the foreign consonant cluster and an emphatic ṭ to carry its weight. The name had its hour of sovereignty: in the 820s a band of Andalusian exiles seized the island and made it an emirate that raided the Aegean for nearly a century and a half, so that *Iqrīṭish* was, until the Byzantine reconquest of 961, the name of a state and not merely a place, with a fleet that made it feared across the Aegean.

On this page the name is the *krete* family's furthest station, and the page's pattern is the island's whole history in miniature. Crete's Bronze Age name, the *Kaptara* and *Kaftor* of the eastern archives, died with the palaces that bore it; the Greek *Krḗtē* replaced it so completely that every later language, Latin, Syriac, Arabic, is only carrying the Greek forward in a different mouth. *Iqrīṭish* shows the replacement holding even under conquest: the Andalusians who took the island from the Greeks took its Greek name with it, emphatics and all. The Minoans' island has been named in five scripts across four thousand years, and for the last three of those millennia every name has been the same word.
