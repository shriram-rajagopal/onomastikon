*Nubaei* is the Latin form of the Nubian name, attested in Pliny's catalogue of the peoples of the Nile above Egypt, compiled from the Greek explorers and from Juba of Mauretania: "from there the Nubaei Aethiopes, eight days' journey; their town, set on the Nile, is Tenupsis" (6.191). The ethnic takes the Latin *-aei* ending that Pliny gives to several of his Ethiopian peoples, and the phrase classes the Nubians as one kind of Ethiopian, a people within the larger category the Greek name had established.

The entry is small, a single line in a catalogue, and it carries the page's two threads nested in one phrase. In *Nubaei Aethiopes* the Nubia name and the Ethiopia name stand side by side, the one inside the other: the Nubians are Ethiopians, as Latin saw it. In the centuries after Meroë the inner term would grow and the outer would migrate, Nubia becoming the name of the middle Nile and Ethiopia the name of Aksum, so that Pliny's phrase, read today, names two different countries. The Latin *Nubaei* is the seed of the medieval and modern Nubia; Pliny planted it as an adjective.
