*cynstn*, conventionally read *Čīnastān*, is the Sogdian name of China, the base *čīn* with the Iranian land-suffix *-stān*: "the land of Chin." It is attested in the Sogdian corpus from the era of the Ancient Letters onward, the merchant correspondence abandoned near Dunhuang around 313 CE, whose writers report from inside the country the famine at the capital, the sack of Ye and Luoyang, and the flight of the emperor; the same letters use the base for the Chinese themselves. The word continues the Sanskrit and Middle Persian *Čīna*/*Čīn* of this family, itself most commonly derived from the name of the Qin.

The form's distinction on this page is its vantage. The Greek and Latin names for China are horizon-words, reports of silk people at the world's edge; the Sogdian word was written by men who lived in Chinese cities and dated their letters by the disasters of Chinese politics. And the suffix mattered more than the base: *-stān* went on to build the map of Asia from Turkestan to Hindustan, and in the Persian *Čīnistān* this exact compound outlived the merchants who first wrote it.
