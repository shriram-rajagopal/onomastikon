*Bursa* (بروسه) is the Turkish form of Prousa, taken with the city at Orhan's capture of 1326 after a decade-long blockade begun under Osman himself; the Arabic-script spelling, Burūsa, keeps the Greek shape visible while the spoken name settled into two Turkish syllables. As the Ottoman state's first real capital the city received the dynasty's first monuments, and it kept two permanent roles after the court moved on: the burial place of the first sultans, Osman and Orhan lying in converted Byzantine buildings above the plain, and the silk city of the empire, its market names, Koza Han, the cocoon caravanserai, recording the trade.

The entry closes the smallest and steadiest family of this cluster. Nothing dramatic ever happened to the name Prousa: no fossilized preposition as at İznik, no eroded emperor as at Edirne, just a Hellenistic king's name passing through Latin audits and Byzantine centuries into Turkish, lightly re-voweled, never challenged. Its distinction is what happened around it, for this ordinary continuation is the name the Ottomans chose to be buried under; the dynasty that would take Constantine's city and keep Constantine's name for it began by keeping Prusias's, the habit of preserving predecessors' names already set at the first capital. Empires advertise their conquests in new names or in kept ones; the Ottomans, from Bursa onward, were keepers.
