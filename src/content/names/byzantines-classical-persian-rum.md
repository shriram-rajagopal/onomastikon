The Classical Persian name for the Roman world, continuing the *Hrōm* and *al-Rūm* of its Middle Persian and Arabic predecessors into the language of the medieval eastern courts. In the *Shāhnāma*, Alexander himself is a prince of *Rūm*, and the word covers Byzantium and its lands throughout classical Persian literature; when the Seljuks took Anatolia they became, in their own Persian chancery, the Sultanate of *Rūm*, Rome ruled by Turks.

The Persian career of the name produced its most famous bearer. A Persian-speaking scholar from Balkh who settled at the Seljuk capital of Konya became known, from his adopted country, as Mawlānā *Rūmī*: the best-read Persian poet is surnamed "the Roman" because he lived in what Persian still called Rome, in a city the Caesars never particularly cherished, under Turkish rule, two centuries before Byzantium fell. No other form on this page shows so plainly that the name had come loose from the city: by the end of the era covered here, *Rūm* meant wherever Rome's ghost was currently residing.
