The Demotic word for Greeks, *Wynn*, the ordinary designation of the Greek-speaking world in Egyptian documents of the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. The form is the Egyptian adaptation of the Ionian name that the Near East at large applied to the Greeks, most likely reaching Egyptian through the Aramaic of Persian-period administration, the same channel that carried the name across the eastern Near East. Its most famous attestation is the Rosetta Stone: where the decree prescribes its own publication in three scripts, the Demotic register calls the Greek script the writing of the *Wynn*, while the hieroglyphic register reaches for the antiquarian *Ḥꜣw-nbwt* and the Greek text says simply "Hellenic letters."

That single stone is the entry's significance in miniature: three registers of one decree name the Greeks by three unrelated traditions, the inherited geographic phrase of the monumental script, the living Ionian loan of the everyday script, and the Greeks' own *Hellēnes*, and all three traditions stand as entries on this page. *Wynn* is also the headwater of Coptic *Uweinin*, which carried Egypt's word for the Greeks into the Christian period, giving the Ionian name an Egyptian-internal arc of its own alongside its Akkadian, Hebrew, and Persian branches. On the very monument that taught the modern world to read Egyptian again, Egypt names the Greeks three ways, and only the Greek version of the name is Greek.
