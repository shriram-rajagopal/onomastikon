The Avestan name of the seven-river country, *Hapta Həṇdu*, the sixteenth and last of the good lands created by Ahura Mazda in the *Vendidad*'s opening catalogue: the Iranian scripture's easternmost horizon, the river-plain this page treats under its Vedic name. The phrase is the exact cognate of Sanskrit *Sapta Sindhu* above it, the same two inherited words for the same country, and the form also stands on the India page, where the *Vendidad*'s frontier-land answers Old Persian *Hinduš* as the name of the wider Indus world.

Set beside its Sanskrit twin, the pair displays the oldest sound-law in this atlas twice in two words: inherited *s* became *h* in Iranian, so Indian *sapta* is Iranian *hapta* and Indian *sindhu* is Iranian *həṇdu*, one phrase pronounced from the two sides of the mountains that divided the Aryan world. Every later name for India in Persian, Greek, Arabic, and English descends from the *h*-side of exactly this equation. The Punjab is where the two traditions stood closest and heard each other best, and its page holds the rare thing itself: the same name, twice, in the two accents of a parted family.
