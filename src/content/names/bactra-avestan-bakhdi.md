*Bāxδī* is the Avestan name of Bactra, listed in the first chapter of the *Vidēvdād* among the sixteen good lands created by Ahura Mazda, with the epithet "the beautiful, with uplifted banners." The land-list does not separate city from country, and *Bāxδī* names the oasis settlement and its territory as one thing, in the oldest Iranian form of the name; the Avestan-script spelling is a Sasanian-era fixing of a much older oral text. On the Bactria page this entry stands for the land; it stands here because later Zoroastrian tradition read the *Vidēvdād*'s Bāxδī as the holy city itself, the seat of Vishtaspa, first royal patron of the prophet.

Within this page's family the Avestan form is the headwater: its *-xδ-* is the cluster that Old Persian rendered *-xt-* (*Bāxtriš*), Greek heard as *-kt-* (*Báktra*), and Middle Persian wore down to the *-x-* of *Baxl*. The banners of the formula have been read as the standards of a prosperous fortified place, which is to say that the oldest attestation of the name already describes a city worth defending; the epithet and the etymology have been argued over for two centuries, and the entry keeps the traditional reading with that caution on record.
