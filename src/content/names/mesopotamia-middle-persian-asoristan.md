The Middle Persian name for Mesopotamia in the Sasanian period, *Āsōristān*, "land of the Assyrians," the old name of Assyria extended with the Iranian land-suffix *-istān*. It stands in the province list of Šābuhr I's trilingual inscription and remains the regular designation of the Mesopotamian province in the Pahlavi administrative and geographical tradition, including the *Šahrestānīhā ī Ērānšahr*. By Sasanian usage the name covered above all the alluvial south around the capital at Ctesiphon, the richest and most populous province of the empire; the same form is treated on the page for Assyria, whose name it carries, and it stands beside the Manichaean *Bābel* recorded under Babylon as the other Middle Persian name for the country between the rivers.

Every other name on this page describes the land: Greek *Mesopotamía* and its Latin and Armenian copies say "between the rivers," Hebrew *Aram-naharaim* and Syriac *Bēth Nahrīn* say "of the rivers," Arabic *al-Jazīra* says "the island." The Sasanian name alone describes no water at all. It names a people, and a vanished one, the Assyrians of the north, whose empire had been dust for eight centuries before Ardashir's tax collectors wrote the word. Where every neighbor mapped Mesopotamia by its geography, the empire that governed it filed it under a ghost.
