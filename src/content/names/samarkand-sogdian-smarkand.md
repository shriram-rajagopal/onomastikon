*smʾrknδh* is Samarkand's name in Samarkand's own language, conventionally read *Smārkanθ*: it appears in the Sogdian Ancient Letters of c. 313, the merchant correspondence found in a watchtower west of Dunhuang, and again in the Mugh documents of the early eighth century, the archive of the last independent Sogdian lord, hidden in a mountain castle from the advancing Arabs. The usual analysis reads the name as *asmara-* "stone" with *kanθ* "town, fort," the stone city, the second element being the same *kand* that closes a whole family of Central Asian town-names, Tashkent among them.

The entry does for Samarkand what *swγδ* does for Sogdiana on its page: it supplies the insider's spelling against which every outsider's version can be measured, and the measurements are instructive. Greek *Marákanda* dropped the initial cluster the Greek mouth disliked; Arabic and Persian *Samarqand* broke it open with a vowel instead; the reconstructed *Smārkanθ* explains both at once, the one name behind the two accommodations. Its two attestation contexts are the Sogdian trading world's alpha and omega, a commercial letter from the network's morning and a fugitive prince's archive from its last day; the name is spelled the same in both. The merchants and the doomed lord agreed on the spelling, and it is their spelling, not the conquerors', that the etymology trusts.
