The direct descendant of Middle Egyptian *Kemet* (𓆎𓅓𓏏), preserving the same root through more than two millennia of linguistic evolution. ⲕⲏⲙⲉ is the Sahidic form; Bohairic and the other Coptic dialects each preserve their own reflex of the name, and it remained the ordinary Coptic word for Egypt throughout the language's literary life, from the earliest biblical translations of the third and fourth centuries CE through its long survival in the liturgy of the Coptic church.

*Kēme* is the final native stage of the oldest naming tradition on this page, and the stage at which the name finally became audible. Hieroglyphic writing recorded the consonants of *km.t* and left its vowels unwritten; Coptic, spelling the word in its Greek-derived alphabet, fixed them on the page for the first time, and the reconstruction of the ancient pronunciation works backward from precisely this form. The name also outlived the political world that coined it: the Copts' own word for their language, ϯⲙⲉⲧⲣⲉⲙⲛ̄ⲭⲏⲙⲓ, "the speech of the people of Kēme," is built on it, so that long after the pharaonic state was gone, the Black Land remained inside the name Egyptian gave itself.
