*Iatribu* (𒅀𒀜𒊑𒁍, ia-at-ri-bu) is the Akkadian name of Yathrib, and the oldest attestation of Medina by more than six centuries. It appears in the Harran stele of Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon, in the list of Arabian oases he controlled during his ten-year residence at Tayma in the 550s BCE: from Tema the text runs through Dadanu, Padakku, Ḫibrâ, and Iadiḫu "as far as Iatribu," the southern limit of a Babylonian king's astonishing detour into Arabia. The spelling renders the same consonantal skeleton, y-t-r-b, that the name has carried ever since.

The entry gives Medina one of the deepest attestation floors of any city in this atlas's later phases, and the position of the name in its source is what makes it memorable. Nabonidus's Arabian sojourn was the strangest episode of the last Mesopotamian empire, a king who left Babylon to its priests and went to live among the caravan oases; Yathrib enters history as the farthest point of that eccentric journey, an endpoint on the itinerary of cuneiform culture's final major king. The list orders the oases from north to south, so the name stands last. The last name in the last great cuneiform king's list of desert towns is the city where, eleven centuries later, the Islamic calendar begins.
