*Marv* (مرو) is the Classical Persian continuation of the name, the same written shape as the Arabic but the native line of descent, Middle Persian *Marw* carried into the reborn literary language with its final consonant softened in speech. In the *Shāhnāma* the city belongs to the epic geography of the northeast, the marchland of the wars with Turan, and it is at Marv that Ferdowsi sets the death of Yazdgird, the epic's actual historical ending, the miller's hut scene rendered into verse within living tradition of the Sasanian catastrophe.

The Persian entry closes the page's chain where the chain began, in an Iranian language, and adds the only thing still missing: mourning. Old Persian recorded the oasis as property, Greek as geography, Arabic as metropolis and then as loss; Persian, whose literature was born in Khurasan among Marv's sister cities, made the city a setting for the death of Iran's last king and kept it in the national poem forever. The modern Turkmen Mary, the name's living form, descends from this *Marv* through Turkic pronunciation. Twenty-five centuries after Behistun the name is still on the ground and still two syllables; empires abbreviate, but the oasis, as ever, keeps what remains.
