*Marguš* (𐎶𐎼𐎦𐎢𐏁) is the Old Persian name of the Merv oasis, attested in Darius's Behistun inscription both in the catalogue of lands and in the narrative of the revolt of 522 BCE, when one Frāda raised Margiana against the new king and Darius's satrap crushed him; the episode gets its own paragraph of the inscription, which is more press than most provinces ever received. The name is the Iranian stem *margu-*, of debated meaning, that every later form of the city's name continues.

The entry's place in the family is the head of the documented chain, though not of the name itself; the Avestan *Mouru* beside it preserves the same word in the older sacred tradition, and neither derives from the other. What Behistun contributes is a date and a politics: the first fixed moment at which the oasis is visible, and visible characteristically, as a rich margin rebelling against a center. Merv would replay that scene for the next two millennia, against Seleucids, Sasanians, and finally as the launching pad of the Abbasid revolution, the province's habit of unmaking empires already rehearsed in its first appearance in writing. The oasis enters history in a royal inscription, listed as property and described as trouble; both notices proved durable.
