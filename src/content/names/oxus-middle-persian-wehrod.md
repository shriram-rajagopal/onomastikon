*Wehrōd*, "the good river," is the name the Zoroastrian tradition gives one of the two great streams of its cosmography: in the *Bundahišn* two great rivers flow out from Alburz, the Arag toward the west and the Weh toward the east, and the Weh passes through the land of Sind and reaches the sea in Hindūstān. The identification is the disputed part, marked accordingly. The *Bundahišn*'s own routing points toward the Indus, while the analysis founded by Markwart reads the older core of the tradition as naming the Oxus, the great river of the Iranian northeast that the Avestan and later entries on this page call *Vaxšu*; on that reading, standard in the reference literature for the Amu Darya, the entry belongs here. The attestation is Book Pahlavi, which has no Unicode encoding, so the entry carries transliteration only.

The uncertainty is not a defect of the sources but the nature of the name. Every other form on this page descends from *Vaxšu*, a real river's own name passed from language to language; *Wehrōd* is instead a role in a cosmological diagram, the good river of the good creation, which the tradition pinned to whatever great eastern water its geography could reach. A name like that can migrate downstream of theology in a way no borrowed toponym can, and its drift between the Oxus and the Indus is the visible wobble of a map drawn from scripture. The exonyms on this page name the river; the tradition's own word names an idea that the river was, for a while, allowed to hold.
