The Greek name for the Nile, attested from Hesiod and Homer onward and the source of the modern European name. The etymology is uncertain; the most common derivation is from Egyptian *nꜣ-jrw* ("the rivers," plural of *jtrw*), though Beekes notes that the Greek form's vocalism is difficult to reconcile with the proposed Egyptian source. Alternative etymologies trace it to Semitic *naḥal* ("river-bed, wadi") or treat it as pre-Greek substrate. Herodotus' *Histories* book 2 devotes extensive discussion to the Nile and is among the earliest detailed Greek accounts.
