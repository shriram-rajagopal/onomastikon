The medieval Latin name for the Arabs and then for the whole Islamic world, *Sarraceni*. The name is older than Islam: Ptolemy's *Sarakēnoí* and Ammianus's *Saraceni* were nomads of the Arabian frontier, and its real etymology is unresolved. Isidore of Seville, writing on the eve of the conquests, recorded the folk explanation his age preferred, that they are "called Saracens either because they claim descent from Sarah" or from a Syrian origin; other writers derived them from Hagar, as *Agareni*, or from Ishmael. When the conquests came, Latin did not coin a name for the new power; it stretched this old tribal label over everything from Cordoba to Samarkand, and in the crusade chronicles *Sarraceni* is simply the standing name of the enemy, as fixed in the *Gesta Francorum* as *Turci*.

The page already holds this entry's exact mirror: Middle Persian *Tāzīg*, one border tribe's name stretched over all Arabs, handed on to Armenian and Chinese. Latin did the same from the opposite direction, and the etymological legend it attached shows what the stretch was for: a genealogy from Sarah or Hagar filed the new civilization into the only universal history the West had, the biblical one, as a nation of Abraham's other line. The name was in the encyclopedias before the caliphate existed, waiting; when Islam arrived, the Latin West did not learn a new word for it, and for eight hundred years it fought, traded with, and translated a civilization it never once called by its own name.
