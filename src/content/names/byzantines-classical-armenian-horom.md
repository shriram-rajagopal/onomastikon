*Hoṙom* (Հոռոմ) is the Classical Armenian word for a Roman of the eastern empire, used in the plural, *Hoṙomkʿ*, for the Byzantines and their state, and in phrases like *ašxarh Hoṙomocʿ*, "the land of the Romans," for the empire itself. The word runs through the classical historians from the early medieval centuries onward and stays in use to the end of the Byzantine period. Its shape gives away its route: Armenian did not take the name from Greek Ῥωμαῖοι, whose rough breathing it could have rendered directly, but from Middle Persian *Hrōm*, breaking the un-Armenian initial cluster *hr-* with an inserted vowel to yield *Hoṙom*, with the trilled *ṙ* regular in Iranian loans.

That borrowed *h-* is the entry's philological cargo. Armenia was Byzantium's neighbor, co-religionist, and frequent province, yet its everyday word for the Romans came through the rival empire, learned from Persian speech during the long Sasanian centuries when Armenia looked both ways across the border; the *Yoynkʿ* entry on the page for Greece took the same road in the opposite direction, an Iranian-mediated name for the Greeks. And inside Armenia the imperial name did work no other form on this page does: because the empire was Chalcedonian, *Hoṙom* came to label Armenians of the Greek rite, so that a family could be *Hoṙom* in confession without any Roman thing about it. Elsewhere on this page Rome's name marks a border on the map; in Armenian it could mark a border inside a village, running between neighbors who disagreed about a council.
