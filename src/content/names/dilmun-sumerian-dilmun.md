The Sumerian name of the Gulf trade-land, *Dilmun*, written with the sign group *NI.TUK* and the place-determinative, a spelling so fixed that it served every later period and language of cuneiform. It is among the very oldest toponyms in existence: the name already appears in the archaic texts of Uruk, at the birth of writing itself around 3200 BCE, before any connected sentence can be read, which makes Dilmun the earliest foreign land that writing records. In historical times it runs through the commercial and royal record of Sumer, from Ur-Nanshe of Lagash, whose inscriptions have the ships of Dilmun carry timber as tribute, through the Ur III merchants' accounts of the Gulf trade. The word's own etymology is unknown; it may well be the island's local name, taken whole into Sumerian mouths.

What sets *Dilmun* apart on its own page is that the name works two registers at once, the ledger and the myth. The same land whose boats appear in tribute lists is, in Sumerian literature, the pure and pristine country of *Enki and Ninhursag*, where no raven croaks and no lion kills, and the place where the gods settled Ziusudra, the flood-hero, to live forever where the sun rises. No other entry in this atlas names a country that its neighbors could both invoice and imagine as paradise without changing the word. Every later name on this page, Akkadian *Tilmun*, very likely Greek *Týlos* too, descends from or continues the name fixed here, at the moment writing began.
