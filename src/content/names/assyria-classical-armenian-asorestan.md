The Armenian name of Assyria, *Asorestan*, the Middle Persian *Asōristān* of this page taken over whole, the Assur-root wearing the Iranian suffix of countries. In Armenian literature the name belongs above all to the national legend: Movsēs Xorenacʿi's first book sets Armenia's founding drama against *Asorestan*, whose queen Šamiram, the Semiramis of the Greeks, desires the beautiful Armenian king Ara, invades when refused, and builds her city and canal by Lake Van. Long after the historical Assyria had fallen, its Armenian name remained the address of the empire next door in the oldest stories Armenians told about themselves.

The entry sits in the *ashur* family at one remove more than most: Armenian did not inherit Assur's name from Assyria but from the empires that succeeded it, taking the Sasanian name of a Sasanian province for a kingdom nine centuries gone. That is why its Assyria is legendary rather than administrative. For the Akkadian and Aramaic entries on this page, Aššur was a state one paid taxes to; for Armenian, *Asorestan* was where Semiramis came from, an antagonist of myth wearing the name of a bureaucratic district. The same word, passed down the centuries, ended as the stage-name of an empire that survived only in stories, and Armenia kept it in use because the stories were its own.
