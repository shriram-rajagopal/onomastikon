*Kordýbē* (Κορδύβη) is the Greek form of the city's name, attested in Strabo's survey of Baetica, where he singles the city out as the foundation of Marcellus and, with Gades, the greatest of the region's cities, famed for the quality of its land and the early Romanization of its people. The form is the Latin *Corduba* passed through Greek phonology, the Latin *u* rendered with upsilon and the ending assimilated to the Greek first declension; Strabo knew southern Iberia through Roman sources and Roman informants, and his spelling records how the Roman name sounded in Greek.

The entry's role on the page is to show the direction of knowledge in the Roman west. In the atlas's eastern theaters Greek is usually the intermediary that carries local names to others; here the roles are reversed, and Greek receives Iberia secondhand from Latin, a province of Rome's knowledge rather than a source of its own. Iberia is one of the few regions of the atlas that the Greek language only ever saw over Roman shoulders, and *Kordýbē*, a Latin word in Greek dress describing a city neither language had named, is what that looks like on the page.
