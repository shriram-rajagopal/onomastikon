The Armenian name of the Euphrates, *Epʿrat*, for the one great river of this atlas that rises inside Armenia itself: both branches of the Euphrates come down from the Armenian highland, and the river ran through Armenian history from the kingdom's western marches to the plain of Awarayr. The form entered written Armenian with the Bible, where Genesis names the river among the four of Eden, and its shape reads like a receipt from two channels at once: the aspirated *pʿ* answers the Greek *phi* of *Euphrátēs*, while the missing Greek ending keeps the outline of the Semitic *Pĕrāt*, and which channel carried it is not settled; it remains the Armenian name today.

Whichever family the form belongs to, the borrowing means the same strange thing. Every other language on this page named a river that flowed toward it or past it; Armenian named the river at its own springs with a word that had traveled four thousand years and two thousand kilometers from Sumer, up the whole length of the stream, to arrive back at the source. The people who lived where the Euphrates begins learned what to call it from the people who lived where it ends. Rivers carry their names downstream as a rule; on this page, the Euphrates' name is the rare one that swam the whole way back up.
