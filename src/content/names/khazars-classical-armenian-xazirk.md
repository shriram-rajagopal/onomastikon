The Classical Armenian *Xazirkʿ*, with the regular Armenian plural, the name of the power beyond the Caucasus in the historians of the empire's most exposed corner. Its richest witness is Movsēs Kałankatuacʿi's history of Caucasian Albania, which records the Khazar irruptions of the seventh century from underneath: the sack of Darband, the siege of Tbilisi, the tribute officers of the qaghan installed over Albanian towns, and the mission of the bishop Israyēl to the huts and holy groves of the north, one of the closest looks at steppe religion any medieval author took. The Armenian Geography lists the *Xazirkʿ* among the peoples of its inherited Sarmatia, the old Ptolemaic frame updated with the steppe's new master, as the Sarmatians' page of this atlas records.

The Armenian name is the earliest in the family's documentary record, in use while Greek and Arabic were still learning whom they faced, because the Caucasus principalities met the qaghanate first and hardest. Its entries are correspondingly unlike the others on this page: not the diplomacy of equals or the geography of savants but the ledger of a small Christian world adjusting to a new roof, tribute assessed, churches bargaining, a bishop sent north to make Christians of the tax collectors. The great powers named the Khazars from across a chessboard; Armenian named them from beneath.
