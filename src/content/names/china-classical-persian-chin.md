The Classical Persian name for China, *Chīn*, continuing Middle Persian *Čīn* into the new stage with its affricate finally at home: the Perso-Arabic script's *che* was invented for exactly such Persian sounds, ending the makeshift by which the older stage had borrowed a Manichaean letter to write its own word. In the *Shāhnāma* the emperor of *Chīn* is a stock figure of the eastern horizon, and the geographers pair the name in the phrase *Chīn u Māchīn*, China and greater China, for the farthest reach of the known world.

The form matters for where it traveled next. Persian was the trade and chancery language of the medieval routes east, and it was largely the Persian shape of this old Sanskrit-Iranian name that later reached European tongues, seeding the forms that become "China" itself. On a page where the *cina* family already runs from the Mahābhārata to the Arabic geographers, the Persian entry is the relay station: the name of the Qin, twenty dynasties gone, still passing westward through Persian mouths, and *Chīn* remains the Persian word for China today.
