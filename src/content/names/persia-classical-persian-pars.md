The New Persian continuation of the name this whole page traces, *Pārs*, the old *Pārsa* of Darius come home to its own language's next age. In the Shāhnāma and the classical literature *Pārs* names the southern homeland province and, in the older manner, the realm itself; beside it stands the arabicized doublet *Fārs*, born because Arabic has no *p* and the conquest-era administration wrote the province's name as it could pronounce it. Persian kept both, the native form in poetry and the Arabic form on the map, and *Fārs* remains the province's official name in Iran today.

The entry closes the family circle in the one language where the name was never a foreign word. Every other member of the *parsa* family, Akkadian, Elamite, Greek, Latin, is the name as heard from outside; *Pārs* is the word's own descendant, passed mouth to mouth from Achaemenid speech into the language of Ferdowsi, who used it to write the national epic of the very dynasty that first carved it in stone. The doublet completes the page's irony: after two and a half millennia of continuous inheritance, the form the world can find on a modern map is the one reshaped by the only conquerors who could not say its first letter.
