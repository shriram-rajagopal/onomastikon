The Middle Persian name of Sogdiana, *Sūgd*, the Old Persian *Suguda* of the Achaemenid satrapy lists contracted by a millennium of Persian speech to a single syllable. It is attested in the great trilingual inscription of Šābuhr I at the Kaʿba-ye Zardušt, c. 262 CE, where the king's dominion runs "up to Kāš, Sūgd, and Čāčestān": Sogdiana stands in the list not as a governed province with a resident administration but as the named limit of empire, the last country before the steppe. The spelling here is the Inscriptional Pahlavi rendering used for Middle Persian in this atlas.

The position in the sentence is the entry's real content. Darius had listed *Suguda* as a possession; Šābuhr names *Sūgd* as a boundary, the point up to which power reaches rather than a place where it sits. Between the two inscriptions the name itself had worn from three syllables to one while its grammatical role wore from object to landmark, the word and the claim eroding together. Within the family the form is the Persian-side twin of what the Sogdians wrote for themselves as *swγδ*, recorded on this page from their own letters; the two spellings, converging from either end of the Iranian world, agree on the shape the name would carry into Arabic as *al-Ṣughd*.
