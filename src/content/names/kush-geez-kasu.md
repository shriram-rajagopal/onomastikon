*Kasu* is the name of Kush in the inscriptions of Ezana of Aksum, the fourth-century king whose titulary runs "king of Aksum and of Ḥimyar and Raydān and Sabaʾ and Salḥēn and Ṣǝyāmo and Bǝga and of Kasu, king of kings." The name stands in the royal style of three of his stones (RIE 187, 188, 189) and in the Greek versions as *Kasou*; in RIE 189, the record of his Nile campaign, the Kasu are a people living at the confluence of the Takkaze and the Seda, the Atbara and the Nile, with towns of stone and straw called Alwa and Daro, which the king's army took. The spelling writes the first syllable in the fourth order, *kā*, and the vowel is the entry's puzzle: every Near Eastern form has a long *u*, and Aksum alone has *a*. Whether the Geʽez name came from the Meroitic kingdom's own word, from the Greek of the Red Sea trade, or from some Semitic form the atlas does not hold, cannot be settled, and the entry carries no edge.

The form is the last contemporary witness. Ezana's stone is the only text in any language that names Kush while a Kushite polity still stood and records the breaking of it, and it names it twice over: in the title, as a land the king claimed, and in the narrative, as a people his army fought on the Nile. It is also the only name for Kush on this page written by another African kingdom. The first stone to name Kush was an Egyptian king's boundary marker; the last was an Aksumite king's victory stone; between them lie two thousand years in which the one word we cannot read is the kingdom's own.
