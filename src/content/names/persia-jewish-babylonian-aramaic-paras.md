The Babylonian Aramaic name of the empire, *Paras*, the form by which the Talmud names the Sasanian state under whose rule it was composed. The spelling continues the biblical *Pāras* of Cyrus and Esther, but the Bavli's Persia is not the distant empire of Scripture; it is the contemporary power, its kings and magi and laws part of the rabbis' own daily world.

*Paras* is the one name of Persia in this atlas given from inside it. Where the Greek, Hebrew, and Latin forms were written by outsiders looking at the Persian empire, the Babylonian rabbis lived within the Sasanian realm and named it as residents, observing the Persians' fire-worship and dress and courts at first hand, sometimes admiringly: "three things I like about the Persians," one sage says. Persia is, for the Talmud, less a foreign nation than the state one paid taxes to and argued law beside.
