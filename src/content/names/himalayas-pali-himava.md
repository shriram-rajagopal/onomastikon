*Himavā* (stem *Himavant*) is the Pali name of the Himalayas, the Middle Indo-Aryan continuation of Sanskrit *Himavat*, "the one possessing snow." In the canon the Himavā is less a mountain range than a mythic north: the abode of ascetics and strange peoples, the source of the great rivers, the standard of immensity. The *Dhammapada* makes it a simile for the good, who "shine from afar like the snowy mountain," and the Jātakas people its slopes with hermits, elephants, and golden geese; cosmological texts count it among the great features of Jambudīpa, the southern continent.

The entry's interest is in register. The Sanskrit tradition on this page names the range in two ways, the possessive *Himavat* and the compound *Himālaya*, "abode of snow"; Pali inherited the first and made it the ordinary word, and it is the possessive form, not the compound that eventually conquered the world's atlases, that the Buddhist canon carried across Asia. A reader of the Jātakas in Burma or Ceylon knew the mountains as the Himavā centuries before any European knew them at all.
