*Yathrib* (يثرب) is the pre-Islamic Arabic name of the oasis, the direct continuation of the y-t-r-b attested in cuneiform and Greek for a thousand years before. It appears in the poetry of the pre-Islamic period and exactly once in the Qur'an, at 33:13, where during the siege of the Trench "a party of them said, O people of Yathrib, there is no stand for you, so return." The name did not survive the events it names. After the hijra the town became the city of the Prophet, and tradition reports that Muhammad disliked the old name and renamed the place Ṭāba or Ṭayba, "the sweet"; in practice what replaced it was *al-Madīna*, and *Yathrib* fell out of living use within a generation.

This is the atlas's clearest case of a name deliberately retired. Names elsewhere fade by conquest or drift; *Yathrib* was set aside by decision, and the decision held so completely that its one Qur'anic occurrence, on the lips of the disheartened during the siege, became the name's last public appearance, preserved forever precisely because scripture cannot be revised. Later piety even read the verse's speakers as making a point, using the obsolete pagan name as a small act of disbelief. Its ancient siblings, *Iatribu* and *Iathrippa*, mark this page's deep past; *Yathrib* marks the moment that past was formally closed. Most names on this atlas died of neglect; this one was retired by decree, and fourteen centuries later the decree still holds.
