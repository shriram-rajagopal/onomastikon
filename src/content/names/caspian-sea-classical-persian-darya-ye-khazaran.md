The Persian name of the sea, *Daryā-yi Khazarān*, "the Sea of the Khazars," with the native *daryā* where the Arab geographers write *baḥr*. The *Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam*, the anonymous geography of 982 composed for a prince of Gūzgānān and the oldest surviving work of Persian geographical prose, describes the sea under this name: the Ghūz to its east, the Khazars to its north, and the Iranian coastlands of Gīlān, Daylamān, Ṭabaristān, and Gurgān along its south. The Persian usage runs parallel to the Arabic *Baḥr al-Khazar*, both naming the sea for the Turkic khaganate that dominated its northern steppe through the early Islamic centuries.

The Khazar khaganate collapsed within a century of the *Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam*, and elsewhere the sea's naming rotation simply moved on, as it always had, to the next people or the next town on the rim. In Persian the rotation stopped. The name outlived the khaganate, the Mongol invasions, and the entire subsequent turnover of the sea's shores, and *daryā-yi Khazar* remains the everyday Iranian name of the Caspian today. The language of the sea's southern coast, with a dozen shore-names of its own to choose from, kept the name of a vanished steppe empire for a thousand years after the last Khazar was gone.
