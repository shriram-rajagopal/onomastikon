The Lycian ethnic "Spartan," the Greek city's name fitted with the native suffix *-zi* that Lycian uses to make a people out of a place. The form *sppartazi* stands fully preserved on the east face of the Xanthos pillar, in a single line that files the Ionians, the Spartans, and the Athenians one after another; the geminate *pp* is the Lycian script's way with the borrowed cluster. The word appears again split across the edge of the stone, *sppart[azi(je)]*, in a clause read as naming a scribe of the king of Sparta, though the restoration there is not complete.

The line that preserves this word is the Peloponnesian War carved in a language neither combatant could read. The Xanthos dynast fought Athens as a client of Persia in the years when Sparta and the Persian king were feeling their way toward the alliance that would end the war, and his pillar names both Greek powers in one breath, enemies and instruments in a single line of ethnics. Greek sources describe that war from inside; the Lycian text is the rare view from the Anatolian shore, where Sparta was not the other great power but one more foreign people to be named with a Lycian suffix and dealt with as the times required.
