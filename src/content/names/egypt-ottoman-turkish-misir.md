*Mısır* (مصر) is the Turkish form of Egypt's Arabic name, written identically to the Arabic and pronounced by Turkish rules: the emphatic consonants and the pharyngeal environment vanish, and an epenthetic vowel splits the cluster, turning one syllable into two with the back vowel harmony intact. The name entered Anatolian Turkish with the rest of the Arabic-Persian learned vocabulary and remains the ordinary modern Turkish word for Egypt; in Ottoman usage it named both the country and, in the old Arabic manner, its capital, and *Mısır Çarşısı*, the Egyptian Bazaar, still stands in Istanbul.

The entry is the eleventh language of the mṣr family and its phonological outlier. Every Semitic member kept the root's emphatic ṣ as a matter of inheritance, and Chinese *Wùsīlí* transcribed the Arabic as best it could; Turkish, with neither emphatics nor a taste for closed clusters, is the first family member to rebuild the name around vowel harmony, the tell that instantly marks the form as Turkish. The family that began in Akkadian border-language and crossed to China by sea thus acquired, at the atlas's very edge, one more empire's accent, and the name of Egypt, four thousand years old and never once replaced, proved able to survive even being pronounced entirely in vowels it had never contained.
