*Media* is the Latin name of the country and *Medus* its inhabitant, taken over from the Greek *Mēdía* with the long vowel shortened in the regular Latin fashion. It is the standard name in Roman geography and history, in Pliny's survey of the lands beyond the Tigris and in the poets, for whom *Medus* could stand by metonymy for the Parthian or Persian east in general. The Roman authors inherited the Greek conflation of Mede and Persian, and *Medus* in Augustan verse is often simply the eastern enemy, the archer beyond the Euphrates.

Through Latin the name reached its final and most durable form, the unchanged *Media* of every modern European language. The route is the well-worn Achaemenid-to-classical channel that also carried Persia and Egypt westward: an Iranian original fixed in Old Persian, rendered into Greek, and handed on through Latin with minimal alteration. That the spelling has not moved in two thousand years is a measure of how early and how firmly the name was set, for a kingdom that ceased to exist before any of these languages wrote it down.
