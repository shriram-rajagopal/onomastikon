*Xwarāsān* is the Middle Persian common noun for sunrise, *xwar* "sun" with the verb of coming, promoted under the Sasanians into the proper name of the empire's eastern quarter: the later empire was administered as four *kusts*, and *kust ī xwarāsān*, the quarter of the sunrise, ran from the central deserts to the Oxus frontier. The word belongs to the Book Pahlavi literature, among it the geographical treatise *Šahrestānīhā ī Ērānšahr*, whose catalogue of the empire's cities opens with the east; Book Pahlavi has no Unicode encoding, so the entry carries transliteration only.

The entry is the birth record of a name that outlived its whole frame of reference. The quarter-system that made "sunrise" a province died with the Sasanians; the name did not, because the Arab conquerors kept the province and its name whole, and Khurasan under the caliphate became more important than it had ever been under the kings who named it. The word's vantage is the entry's quiet content: sunrise from where? From Ctesiphon, from the Mesopotamian center, so that every later use of the name, by Arabs, Turks, and the Khurasanis themselves, silently adopts the point of view of an empire gone since the seventh century. The east is only east of somewhere; Khurasan is east of a capital that no longer exists.
