The Classical Arabic name for Armenia, in use from the conquests of the 640s onward and fixed as the name of a province of the caliphate governed from Dabīl, the city the Armenians called Dvin. Al-Balādhurī's ninth-century *Futūḥ al-Buldān* devotes a chapter to the conquest of *Armīniya* and records the terms under which the country entered Arab rule; Yāqūt's geographic dictionary treats it at length, subdividing the province in the administrative manner of the geographers. The form most likely reached Arabic through Greek and Syriac usage, and carries the Arabic feminine ending on the same *Armina* stem attested a millennium earlier at Behistun.

The entry's significance lies in the company it keeps. Old Persian *Armina*, Greek *Armenía*, Latin *Armenia*, and Arabic *Armīniya* are four successive empires filing the same name for the same highland, each administering a country whose own people called it *Haykʿ* the entire time. No other entry on this page shows the exonym's institutional momentum so plainly: the caliphate inherited the name the way it inherited the roads. A name can pass from empire to empire like a provincial seal, and the people it describes may never once use it.
