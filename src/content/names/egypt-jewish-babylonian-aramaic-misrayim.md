The Babylonian Aramaic name of the land, *Miṣrayim*, continuing the Hebrew dual form unchanged, the Egypt of the Exodus that the Talmud rehearses above all in the laws and the liturgy of Passover. In the Bavli's treatment of the Haggadah, *Miṣrayim* is named in the yearly obligation of each person to "see himself as though he had gone out of Egypt."

*Miṣrayim* belongs to the great Northwest Semitic family of names for Egypt, the *mṣr* root that runs from Akkadian through Hebrew and Aramaic to Arabic. What the Babylonian Aramaic form adds is its setting: in the Talmud, Egypt is less a country on the map than the archetype of bondage, the house of slaves from which redemption is measured. It is named most often not to locate a place but to mark the thing that freedom is freedom from.
