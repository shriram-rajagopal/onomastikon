*Samarqand* (سمرقند) is the Arabic form of the Sogdian name, in use from the conquest of the early eighth century, the initial cluster opened with a vowel and the final element regularized to *-qand*. Arabic tradition attached one great story to the city: that the prisoners taken at the battle of Talas in 751 included Chinese papermakers, and that paper entered the Islamic world through Samarqand's workshops, which remained famous for it; whatever the anecdote's precision, Samarqandi paper was real, celebrated, and consequential, the vehicle on which Arabic book culture scaled. Yāqūt records the city's splendor and its already-legendary antiquity; the name continues unchanged in Arabic to the present.

Within the family the Arabic entry is the great transmitter: it is this vocalized form, *Samarqand*, that New Persian shares, that the Turkic world inherited, and that Europe eventually received, so the modern name of the city descends through the Arabic accommodation of the Sogdian cluster rather than the Greek one. The paper story gives the entry its second layer, since a city whose name means "stone fort" became synonymous with the most fragile of materials; the fort endured sieges, and the paper carried the civilization. Samarqand exported the medium on which its own name would be written for the next thousand years, which is as close to naming immortality as commerce comes.
