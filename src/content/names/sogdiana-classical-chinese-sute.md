The Chinese name of Sogdiana, 粟特 *Sùtè*, the standard form of the dynastic histories from the *Weishu* onward for the country of the oasis merchants who ran the caravan trade into China itself. The transcription renders the Iranian *Suγδ* that this page traces from the Avesta forward; an earlier and garbled form, 粟弋 *Sùyì*, already stands in the *Hou Hanshu*, showing the name had reached Chinese ears centuries before the classic spelling settled. Beside the country-name, Tang administrative practice knew the Sogdian city-states individually as the "nine families of Zhaowu," the *Ānguó* and *Kāngguó* of this atlas's Bukhara and Samarkand pages.

No other language on this page named Sogdiana from so intimate an acquaintance. The Persians taxed it, the Greeks garrisoned it, the Arabs conquered it; the Chinese lived with it, for the Sogdians were the merchants in every western market of Chang'an, and the name *Sùtè* denoted not a distant province but the homeland of neighbors. It is the *suguda* family's furthest station and its most familiar: the country's name carried east not by an army or a scripture but by the Sogdians themselves, who were their own best ambassadors and, for a few centuries, the connective tissue of Asia.
