*Ḥbśt*, read *Ḥabašat*, is the fourth of the nine lands in Ezana's royal title, between Raydān and Sabaʾ in the trilingual inscription of his pagan years (RIE 185). The Geʽez version there is unvocalized, four consonants in the bare letters of the script, and the atlas writes it so, with the reading carried in the asterisked transcription; the pseudo-Sabaic version writes *ḥbs²tm* with the South Arabian ending, and the Greek version, at the same place in the list, writes *Aithiópōn*, "of the Ethiopians." The name is older than Ezana and foreign to his language in origin: the Sabaic inscriptions of Yemen had called Gadarat king of *ḥbs²t* a century and a half earlier, and whether the word began as a South Arabian name for the highlanders, or earlier still as an Egyptian one, is argued; what is certain is that the Aksumite kings took it into their own titulary as one of the things they were king of.

The entry is the Geʽez end of the name that became Abyssinia, and the stone it stands on is the Rosetta of this page. In a single inscription the kingdom's three names sit in three columns: the Aksum of the city, the Ḥabašat of the Arabian neighbors, and, where the Greek translator had to render Ḥabašat, the Aithiopía of the Greek Bible, equated for the first time with the highlands rather than the Nile. Ezana's scribes did not comment on the equation; they simply made it, and the Greek name of Kush became the Greek name of Aksum in the act of translating a royal title.
