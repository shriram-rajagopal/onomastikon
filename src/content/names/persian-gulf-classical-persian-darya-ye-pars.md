The Persian name of the gulf, *Daryā-yi Pārs*, "the Sea of Pars," with the native *daryā* where the Arab geographers write *baḥr*: the *Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam*, the oldest surviving Persian geography, describes it among the world's great seas, the water that takes its name from the province on its northern shore. The name is the Persian half of a pair with the Arabic *Baḥr Fāris* beside it on this page, the same designation in the two languages of the medieval Islamic world, and it continues in the modern Iranian *Khalīj-i Fārs*.

Within the page's long history the entry marks the moment the sea's name came ashore. The Sumerians and Babylonians had named it by position, the Lower Sea against the Upper; the Greeks named it for the empire they sailed against. The Persian form belongs to the people of the coast itself, naming their own water for their own province, and it is the only name on this page in a language spoken on the gulf's shore when the name was coined. The sea has carried the province of Pars in its name for over a thousand years in two languages; the *Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam*'s entry is where the owner's copy of the name begins.
