*Māday* is the Hebrew name for both the Medes and Media, appearing first in the Table of Nations as a son of Japheth (Genesis 10:2) and thereafter as one of the great eastern powers of biblical history. It is the kingdom to which the northern tribes of Israel are deported (2 Kings 17:6), the destroyer the prophets summon against Babylon (Isaiah 13:17; Jeremiah 51:11), and one half of the paired empire "the Medes and the Persians" whose unalterable law runs through Daniel and Esther. The book of Daniel even personifies Media as the silver of the great statue and one of the beasts of its visions.

It is the biblical pairing that gives Media its long afterlife in Western memory. "The law of the Medes and the Persians, which altereth not" became a proverb, and the order of the words, Medes first, preserves the historical seniority of the older empire that Cyrus absorbed. The Hebrew form takes the Iranian name through the same Semitic channel as the Akkadian *Mādāya*, but it is through scripture, not the Assyrian annals, that this particular spelling reached every later European language that reads the Bible.
