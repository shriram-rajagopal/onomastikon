*Sairima-* stands in the Frawardīn Yašt's survey of the world, which honors the souls of the righteous in five lands: the Aryan, the Turian, the Sairimian, the Sainian, and the Dahian. The name is thus one of the Avesta's primordial peoples, and later Zoroastrian tradition made its eponym, as *Salm*, one of the three sons of the primeval king Frēdōn among whom the world was divided, the westerner beside Tūr the northerner and Ēraj the Iranian. The identification of *Sairima-* with the Sarmatians, resting on the phonetic match and on the name's westward assignment, has been standard since the nineteenth century and remains, like most Avestan ethnography, beyond proof; the disputed label carries that doubt. The script rendering is the letter-by-letter normalization from the transliteration that this atlas uses for Avestan.

If the identification holds, this is by far the oldest form on the page, an Iranian people naming Iranian cousins centuries before any Greek wrote *Sauromátai*, and the only entry here from inside the steppe world's own linguistic family. It would also make the page's subject one of the five peoples into which Zoroastrian scripture divided all humanity, an extraordinary rank for nomads the settled world treated as periphery. The doubt itself is worth keeping visible: the Sarmatians' best claim to a place in another people's scripture hangs on two syllables and a direction.
