The Chinese name of Alexandria in Zhao Rukuo's *Zhufanzhi* of c. 1225, recorded as a city of the country of *Wùsīlí*, Egypt. The characters render the Arabic name of the city, *al-Iskandariyya*, as it reached Quanzhou in the speech of the Muslim traders who were Zhao's sources; his chapter preserves a garbled but recognizable account of the Pharos, the great tower on the shore where watch was kept against western fleets, three centuries after the lighthouse itself had begun to fall.

The entry is the family's longest single hop. Alexander's name, wrapped in the Arabic article and worn by Persian and seaborne transmission, crossed the whole of the Indian Ocean trade to be filed in a Chinese gazetteer, the tenth language and easternmost script on this page. It kept, through all of it, the shape of the conqueror's name, which means a Song official in Fujian was writing a distant echo of Macedonian Greek without any way of knowing it. The city of Alexander was known at the ends of the earth by a name that still remembered him, in a country that had never heard of him.
