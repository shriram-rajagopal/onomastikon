*Iáthrippa* (Ἰάθριππα) is the Greek name of Yathrib, carried in Ptolemy's catalogue of inland Arabian towns from the mid-second century and still known to Stephanus of Byzantium's geographical lexicon in the sixth, on the eve of the city's transformation. The transmission of Ptolemy's text also preserves the variant *Lathrippa*. Beneath the Greek dress, a prothetic vowel to open the cluster and a Hellenizing *-ippa* termination, the Arabian name is plainly visible: the consonants y-th-r-b arrive intact, matching both the cuneiform *Iatribu* six centuries earlier and the Arabic *Yathrib* five centuries later.

The entry earns its keep as the methodological counterweight to *Makóraba* on the Mecca page. Both names sit in the same chapter of the same Alexandrian geography, both were identified with holy cities of the Hijaz; but where Makoraba shares little more than an initial consonant with Makka, Iathrippa wears Yathrib's entire skeleton, and so one identification is secure while the other is dissolving under scrutiny. The pair make a clean lesson in what toponymic identification actually requires, displayed across the two holiest cities of Islam. Ptolemy mapped both oases of the future faith; only the one whose consonants survived his Greek can be proven to be itself.
