*Gaṅgā* is the Pali name of the Ganges, the Middle Indo-Aryan continuation of Sanskrit *Gaṅgā*, unchanged in this case because the word had no clusters for the vernacular to smooth. The river runs through the Pali canon as its central geography: the Buddha's career unfolds in the kingdoms along its middle course, and the *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* has him cross the Gaṅgā at the village of Pāṭaligāma while the ministers of Magadha are still building the fortress there that will become Pāṭaliputra. In the canon's stock phrases the river is the measure of number itself, "as many as the sands of the Gaṅgā."

Among the names on this page, the Pali form is notable precisely for not differing from the Sanskrit: where *Takṣaśilā* became *Takkasilā*, the river's name passed between registers untouched. What the canon adds is not a new form but a new relation to the name, the river as lived route rather than sacred abstraction; monks and merchants in these texts board ferries, reckon distances by its fords, and count its sands. The Greek *Gánges* that Megasthenes carried west reproduces exactly the form the Buddha's followers spoke, and the name has not changed since.
