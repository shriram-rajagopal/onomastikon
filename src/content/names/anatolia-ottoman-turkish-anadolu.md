*Anadolu* (اناطولی) is the Turkish name of Anatolia, the Greek *Anatolḗ*, "sunrise, the east," taken into the speech of the Turks who settled the peninsula and made permanent in Ottoman administration: the beylerbeylik of Anadolu, organized in the late fourteenth century, was one of the empire's two founding provinces, the Asian half answering Rumeli's European one. The Arabic-script spelling preserves the learned Arabic form of the name with its emphatic consonant while the pronunciation follows Turkish vowel harmony, and the name continues today as the ordinary Turkish word for the peninsula.

The entry gives the anatole family its neatest closing symmetry, and this phase of the atlas its third sunrise. The Greeks named the peninsula "east" facing it from the Aegean; the Turks who came from the actual east adopted the word and made it their homeland's name, so that modern Turkey's Asian territory is called "the sunrise" in Greek as heard from the wrong side. The Ottoman pairing completed the compass: Anadolu and Rumeli, the east-land and the Roman-land, a Greek direction and a Latin people naming the two halves of a Turkish empire. Khurasan and the Maghreb, elsewhere in this phase, are sunrise and sunset named from a center; Anadolu is a sunrise inhabited by people for whom it lay west, the borrowed name outvoting the map.
