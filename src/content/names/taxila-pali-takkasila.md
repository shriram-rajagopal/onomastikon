*Takkasilā* is the Pali form of the city's name, the Sanskrit *Takṣaśilā* with its cluster assimilated to the doubled *kk* and its sibilants leveled, the regular Middle Indo-Aryan treatment. In the Pali canon and above all in the Jātakas, Takkasilā is a fixed narrative address: the place where young brahmins and princes go to learn the arts under a "world-famed teacher," a stock phrase the tales repeat by the dozen. The city thus functions in Buddhist literature the way Athens functions in Roman literature, as the university town of the imagined past.

The philological interest of the form is its distance from the city's politics. The Jātakas were compiled far to the southeast, and their Takkasilā is a literary memory, a byword for education at the edge of the known world; the tales barely notice the Persians, Greeks, and Kushans who actually ruled it. Meanwhile the everyday spelling in the city's own region, the Gāndhārī *Takhaśilā* of Kharoṣṭhī inscriptions, shows a third treatment of the awkward cluster; that language awaits its place in this atlas, and the Pali form stands here for the vernacular branch of the family.
