The Armenian name of the sea, *Kaspicʿ cov*, "sea of the Caspians": *Kaspicʿ* is the genitive plural of *Kaspkʿ*, the Armenian name of the Caspii, so the phrase means exactly what the Greek *Kaspía Thálassa* means, built instead from Armenian's own grammar. The name appears in the *Ašxarhacʿoycʿ*, the seventh-century Geography attributed to Anania Širakacʿi, which surveys the sea and the lands around it in the fullest geographical work to survive from early medieval Armenia. For Armenians the Caspii were not a people known only from books: their old country near the mouths of the Kura and Araxes lay within the historical province of Pʿaytakaran, where *Kaspkʿ* served as a district name.

Every other name in the *kaspia* family reached its language through the Greek geographic tradition; the Armenian form alone did not need the loan. Armenia bordered the Caspians' country, absorbed it as a province, and inflected their name with its own genitive plural, so that the sea is named "of the Caspians" in living grammar rather than by an inherited adjective. The Caspii themselves disappeared from history in antiquity, yet modern Armenian still says *Kaspicʿ cov*: of all the languages on this page, the one that knew the eponymous people as neighbors is the one that has never stopped declining their name.
