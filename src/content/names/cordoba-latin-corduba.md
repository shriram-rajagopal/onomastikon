*Corduba* is the Latin name of the city from its Roman refoundation in the mid-second century BCE, when the general Marcellus settled it as one of the first Roman communities in Iberia; Pliny lists it as Colonia Patricia, capital of the province of Baetica. The name itself is older than Rome and is generally taken as native Iberian or possibly Punic, its meaning unrecovered; Rome, as usual in the peninsula, latinized what it heard rather than renaming. As the birthplace of the two Senecas and of Lucan, Corduba appears across imperial literature, a provincial capital that exported Latin letters to the capital itself.

Within the page's chain this is the anchor form, the earliest recorded shape of a name that was already old when Latin met it. Its structural interest is that Corduba stands at the head of a transmission it did not travel: the modern name descends from this form only through Arabic *Qurṭuba*, so that the Latin stage, the longest and best documented, is a middle link that later Spanish never touched directly. The same city that sent Seneca to Rome would later send Averroes to Europe, and its name made the same kind of journey, out of one civilization's mouth and home through another's.
