The classical name in its reduced medieval employment: the theme of Hellas, the Byzantine province organized around the end of the seventh century to cover central Greece, with its seat in time at Thebes. Constantine VII catalogues it among the European themes, and the administrative documents of the middle period use *Hellás* in exactly this shrunken sense, one district among dozens, its governor a middling official.

The contraction is the story. *Hellás* had named the whole Greek world; in the empire of the Greeks it named a single province, because the Byzantines called themselves *Rhōmaîoi* and had ceded *Héllēn* to mean pagan, the old national name made theologically unwearable. So the name of the nation survived the Middle Ages as a provincial address, kept on the books by clerks while the people it once named refused it. It waited there a thousand years; when a modern nation went looking for its name in 1821, it found *Hellás* preserved in the one register that never cared what the word meant, the administrative one.
