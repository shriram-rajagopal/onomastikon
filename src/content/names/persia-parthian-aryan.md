*ʾryʾn*, read *Aryān*, is the Parthian name for Iran, attested in the Parthian versions of the early Sasanian royal inscriptions. In the trilingual inscription of Šāpur I at the Kaʿba-ye Zardošt (c. 260 CE), the king's Parthian titulature runs *šāhān šāh Aryān ud Anaryān*, "king of kings of Iran and non-Iran," where the Middle Persian text has *Ērān ud Anērān* and the Greek *Arianōn*; the same Parthian text styles Šāpur lord of *Aryānšahr* (*ʾryʾnḥštr*), the realm of the Iranians. The form recurs in the Parthian version of Narseh's inscription at Paikuli (c. 293 CE), and it appears already in the bilingual inscription on the investiture relief of Ardašir I at Naqsh-e Rustam, where the Parthian line calls him *šāhān šāh Aryān* beside the Middle Persian *Ērān*. Like the Middle Persian form, the word descends from the old Iranian genitive plural *aryānām*, "of the Aryans," and the Greek rendering *Arianōn* in the trilingual confirms the *a*-vocalism of the Parthian reading.

The pairing with Middle Persian *Ērān* is exact, and it mirrors the pairing of Parthian *frwm* with Middle Persian *Hrōm* in which the two Middle Iranian languages name Rome on the same stone. Parthian preserves the older shape: where Middle Persian has contracted the first syllable to *ēr-*, *Aryān* keeps the *arya-* of the Achaemenid royal proclamations audibly intact, so the Parthian line of the inscription is the one in which Darius's old self-designation, "Aryan, of Aryan stock," can still be heard. The conception of Iran as a single named realm appears to be a Sasanian creation; no Arsacid document yet known names the empire this way, and the Arsacids' own coinage long styled its kings with Greek titles. The language of the Parthian dynasty thus first names Iran in monuments commissioned by the dynasty that overthrew it, lending its old word for the Aryans to a political idea its own kings never proclaimed.
