The Armenian name conventionally identified with China, *Čenkʿ*, the land of the *Čen*, with *Čenastan* and *Čenacʿ ašxarh* as fuller variants. Its most famous attestation is genealogical: Movsēs Xorenacʿi tells how a noble named Mamgon fled the king of *Čenkʿ* and settled in Armenia, founding the Mamikonean house, the greatest military dynasty of Armenian history, which thereafter claimed descent from the far east; Xorenacʿi's praise of that homeland, wealthy, peaceable, and devoted to learning, reads like a rumor of China written down at the far end of the road. The form aligns with the Iranian *Čīn*, and through it with the *cina* family that names China across this atlas. The identification itself is the disputed point: a strand of scholarship reads *Čenkʿ* not as China but as a Central Asian country nearer the Jaxartes, and the texts are too far from the referent to settle it.

Whichever land Mamgon actually left, the entry records something rare on this page: a name for China used not to place silk or mark a map's edge, but to found a family. The Greeks and Romans named China for its exports; the Armenians attached it to a pedigree, so that for a thousand years the marshals of Armenia were, by their own account, princes of *Čenkʿ*. It is the only name on China's page that someone claimed as an inheritance, and it was treasured precisely because no one could check: the perfect ancestral homeland is the one at the end of the world.
