*ywnyg*, read *yōnīg*, is the Parthian adjective "Greek," attested in the Manichaean Parthian texts from Turfan that narrate the early history of Mani's church, alongside the adverb *ywnʾw* (*yōnāw*), "in the Greek language." The stem *yōn-* is the regular Parthian outcome of Old Iranian *yauna-*, the "Ionian" word by which the Achaemenid inscriptions had named the Greeks, with the old diphthong contracted to *ō*; and because the Manichaean script writes vowels, the reading is secure. A free-standing Parthian name for Greece as a country is not attested: the surviving corpus, inscriptional and Manichaean alike, preserves the word only as adjective and adverb.

Among the many descendants of the Ionian name gathered on this page, the Parthian form is distinctive for what it is not. Old Persian *Yauna* names a province to be listed, Hebrew *Yāwān* a nation among the nations, Middle Persian *Yunān* a heritage of philosophers; Parthian *yōnīg* never names a place at all. Its attestations sit in the church-historical literature of the Manichaeans, a missionary community for whom what mattered about the Greeks was above all their language, the medium into which a scripture might be carried westward. Greece survives in Parthian not as a country on a map but as a way of speaking.
