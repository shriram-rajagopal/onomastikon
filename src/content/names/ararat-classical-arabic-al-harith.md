The Arabic geographers' name for the great mountain of Armenia, paired with a diminutive for its companion: *al-Ḥārith* the greater peak and *al-Ḥuwayrith* the lesser, the pairing standardly identified with Greater and Lesser Ararat. Al-Qazwīnī's cosmography reports the two mountains of Armenia that no one is able to climb, and Ibn al-Faqīh attaches to them the legend of the thousand cities of the Araxes valley destroyed for rejecting their prophet, with the two peaks hurled there from al-Ṭāʾif. The names themselves are ordinary Arab personal names, a man's name and its diminutive, as if the two summits were an elder brother and a younger.

What is missing from the Arabic name is the flood. Every biblical tradition on this page bends toward the ark; the Qurʾan sets the ark's landfall not here but on al-Jūdī, far to the south, the same southward displacement the Syriac *Qardū* entry records. Relieved of scripture, Ararat needed no sacred name in Arabic and received a human one instead. The mountain that Genesis made the most famous landfall on earth carries, in the language of the geographers who could see it from the caravan roads, a name with no flood in it at all.
