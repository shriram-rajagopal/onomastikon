*Tabγač* is the Old Turkic name of China, the standing term of the Orkhon inscriptions, whose narrative turns on the Türk people's fifty years of subjection to the *Tabγač* emperor and their liberation from him. The name is not Chinese at all in origin: it continues the Tuoba, the Xianbei clan that ruled northern China as the Northern Wei in the fourth through sixth centuries, so that the steppe's word for China preserves the dynasty of steppe conquerors who had most recently mastered it. The form spread widely along the inner Asian routes, and its reflexes served as a name for China in texts far to the west.

Among the names on China's page, this one uniquely reverses the direction of sight: it is China as the steppe saw it, named for the last nomads to take it rather than for any Chinese state's own designation. By the time the inscriptions were carved, the Tuoba were long sinicized and gone, and Tang emperors bore no relation to them; the steppe went on calling China by the ghost of its own emigrants. The entries around this one show China named from its dynasties and its silk; *Tabγač* shows it named, from the north, as a place in the steppe's own family history, the throne a nomad clan once held and lost.
