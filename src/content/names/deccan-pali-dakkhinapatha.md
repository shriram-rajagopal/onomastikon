*Dakkhiṇāpatha* is the Pali form of the name of the south, the Middle Indo-Aryan continuation of Sanskrit *Dakṣiṇāpatha* with the cluster *kṣ* assimilated to *kkh* in the vernacular manner. The canon's most vivid attestation opens the Pārāyana-vagga of the *Sutta Nipāta*: the brahmin Bāvari has left Kosala and settled far down the southern road, on the bank of the Godhāvarī, and his sixteen pupils must walk the whole route back north to question the Buddha, an itinerary the text lists town by town. The name is the route and the region at once, exactly as in Sanskrit.

The Pali form is the one that traveled. When the Greek merchant-author of the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* recorded the country behind the western ports as *Dachinabades*, the shape he heard, with its doubled stop and its *kh*, was the Middle Indo-Aryan one, this form and not the Sanskrit; the entry for that Greek name on this page descends from the word as the canon spells it. The vernacular of Buddhist monks and coasting traders, not the language of the court, is what reached the sea.
