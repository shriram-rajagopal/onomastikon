The Syriac name of the sea, *yammā d-Qazbīn*, "the Sea of Qazvin," named for the Iranian city that lies inland of its southwestern corner, behind the Elburz mountains. The name belongs to the late Syriac geographical tradition, above all to Bar ʿEbroyo, the thirteenth-century Syriac Orthodox maphrian whose learned compendia drew the marine geography of the Syriac schools together with that of the Arabic geographers; it mirrors the *Baḥr Qazwīn* that appears among the sea's many names in the Arabic tradition. The older Aramaic world had lain out of sight of the Caspian, and Syriac inherited no name for it to continue.

For the rivers and lands of its Mesopotamian home, Syriac reaches back through Aramaic to names older than the alphabet, *Bethnahrin*, *Deqlath*, *Perāt*; for the Caspian it had nothing, and when its scholars finally needed a name they adopted their neighbors' newest convention, naming the sea for a famous city near the coast. Every other name on this page points at the water from its rim, a people or a province on the shore; this one points at a city from which the sea cannot even be seen. It is the label of scholars working at second hand, and honest about it: the sea named from a library, by the landmark on the road that led there.
