*Armina* is the oldest securely dated form of the name Armenia, carved into the Behistun inscription of Darius I around 520 BCE, where Armenia appears both among the lands the king rules and among the provinces that rebelled in the first year of his reign. Darius records sending his general Dādṛšiš and fighting several battles "in Armina" before the province was subdued. The form recurs in the later lists of subject peoples at Persepolis and Naqš-e Rustam, fixing *Armina* as the Achaemenid administrative name of the highland satrapy.

This is the headwater of the exonym by which the whole world outside Armenia has known the country ever since. The name's origin before Behistun is obscure, with no certain connection to the Armenians' own self-designation; what is clear is that it is the Old Persian form that travels, into Greek as *Armenía* and onward through Latin. In the trilingual itself the name is given three faces: beside this Old Persian *Armina* stand the Elamite *Harminuya* and, in the Babylonian column, the older highland name *Uraštu*, the Persian chancellery binding its new province to the kingdom of Urartu it had replaced.
