*al-Turk*, with the collective *Atrāk*, is the Arabic name of the Turks, in use from the moment the conquests reached Transoxiana at the end of the seventh century and the armies of the caliphate met the western wing of the qaghanate along the Oxus and Jaxartes. Al-Ṭabarī's history is dense with the name through the campaigns of Qutayba and the long wars against the Türgesh; the geographers made *bilād al-Turk*, the land of the Turks, a standing region of the world, and the lexicographers gave the word an entry beside the oldest words in the language.

No borrowing in this family had stranger consequences for the borrower. Within two centuries of learning the name, the caliphate was recruiting Turkish slave soldiers by the thousand; within three, Turkish generals made and unmade caliphs; and the later medieval Islamic world, from Cairo to Delhi, was ruled largely by dynasties the Arabic language filed under this word. The Persian *Tāzīg* on the Caliphate's page records the Iranian world naming the Arabs who conquered it; *al-Turk* is the mirror image, the conquerors naming the steppe people who would, in the end, inherit their empire.
