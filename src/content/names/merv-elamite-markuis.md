The Elamite rendering of Margiana at Bisitun, *Markuiš*, written *mar-ku-iš*: the Old Persian *Marguš* transcribed with the devoicing of *g* to *k* that Elamite writing regularly applies to Iranian names, and with the sibilant ending kept intact. Like its Babylonian sibling *Marguʾ* on this page, its attestation is a battle narrative rather than a bare list entry, for the Elamite column carries the full account of Frāda's Margian revolt and its suppression in Darius's first year.

The entry completes the oasis's Achaemenid trilingual, and with it a small pattern worth noticing: the three chancelleries each damaged the name differently. Persian kept its own *Marguš* whole; Babylonian let the final sibilant fall away to an aleph; Elamite kept the sibilant but hardened the *g*. One name, three official spellings, no two alike, all carved into the same mountainside describing the same rebellion. Merv's six-form chain across two and a half millennia here passes through its narrowest gate, a single inscription in which the empire itself could not quite agree on the word, and the family survived the disagreement without noticing it.
