The Chinese name for Egypt in the *Zhufanzhi*, the description of the lands of the maritime trade compiled c. 1225 by Zhao Rukuo, customs inspector at the port of Quanzhou. The characters transcribe the Arabic *Miṣr*: Zhao's knowledge of the far west came from Muslim merchants, and his gazetteer records the country of *Wùsīlí* with its great river that rises or fails to rise, an unmistakable report of the Nile's flood, along with its dependent city of *Ègēntuó*, Alexandria.

With this entry the page's oldest family reaches its most distant script. The Semitic *mṣr* root left Akkadian in the second millennium BCE, passed through Hebrew, Ugaritic, Phoenician, Aramaic, and Arabic, and here, four hundred characters deep in a Song customs official's gazetteer, it surfaces in Chinese, transcribed from the speech of dockside informants a hemisphere from the Nile. No other form in the family traveled farther or arrived later. A Bronze Age border-word for Egypt ended its documented ancient journey in a Chinese port ledger, still recognizably itself.
