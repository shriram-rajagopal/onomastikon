The Coptic name of Jerusalem, *Ierousalēm*, the Greek Bible's indeclinable form carried into the Coptic scriptures with the Bohairic dialect prefixing its article, *Thierousalēm*. The form saturates the Coptic New Testament, the martyrdoms, and the liturgy, and in the manuscripts it belongs to a select company: Coptic scribes inherited from Greek the practice of the *nomina sacra*, writing the most holy names as abbreviations under a stroke, and Jerusalem stands in that canon, contracted on the page beside the names of God, Jesus, and the Spirit.

That scribal habit is the entry's distinction, unique on this page. Every language here has a name for Jerusalem; Coptic is the one whose manuscripts marked the name itself as sacred, granting a city the graphic treatment otherwise reserved for the divine. A reader of a Sahidic gospel meets Jerusalem not as a word but as a monogram, a compressed sign that assumes everyone knows what lies inside it. The page's names elsewhere argue about foundation and holiness in their etymologies; the Coptic name makes the argument typographically, holiness asserted not in what the name says but in how it is permitted to appear.
