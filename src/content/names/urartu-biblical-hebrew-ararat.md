*Araraṭ* is the Hebrew form of the Assyrian *Urarṭu*, and in the Bible it names the kingdom as a real northern power, not yet a mountain. Jeremiah summons "the kingdoms of *Araraṭ*, Minni, and Ashkenaz" against Babylon (51:27), listing Urartu beside Mannea and the Scythians among the peoples of the north; the assassins of Sennacherib flee to "the land of *Araraṭ*" (2 Kings 19:37). The Hebrew preserves the Assyrian name with the loss of the initial vowel and a softening of the final consonant, transmitting it through the same Mesopotamian world in which the prophets placed their geography.

On this page *Araraṭ* is the exonym's furthest reach in time, the form in which a vanished Iron Age kingdom outlived itself in scripture. The same Hebrew word, in "the mountains of *Araraṭ*" where the ark comes to rest (Genesis 8:4), later detached from the kingdom and fastened onto a single peak, the snow-capped cone now called Mount Ararat. That narrowing, from a country spanning the highland to one mountain among several candidates, is treated on its own page; here the point is the start of the chain, the moment the Assyrians' name for Urartu entered the book that would carry it to the whole world.
