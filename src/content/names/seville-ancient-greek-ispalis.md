*Híspalis* (Ἵσπαλις) is the Greek form of the city's name, recorded by Strabo in the same survey of Baetica that carries *Kordýbē*: he names it among the notable cities of the Baetis valley, a Roman colonia and river emporium whose trade reached the ocean. The form is a near-transparent transfer of the Latin, the initial aspirate kept as the rough breathing and the shape otherwise unchanged; like everything Strabo knew of inner Iberia, it came through Roman channels.

The entry is the quietest on the page, and its modesty is the point worth recording. Greek, which across the eastern half of this atlas mints names, mediates them, and hands them on to whole families of languages, is in Iberia purely a mirror, reflecting Roman knowledge without adding to it; no later language took the city's name from the Greek form, and the transmission chain that produced modern Sevilla ran from Latin through Arabic without touching it. On the page it is the control case: the same name at the same date in a language with no stake in the place, showing what the name looked like before Arabic went to work on it.
