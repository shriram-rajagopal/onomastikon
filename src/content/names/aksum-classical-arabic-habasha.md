*al-Ḥabasha* is the Arabic name of the Aksumite kingdom and of the country that succeeded it, the South Arabian *ḥbs²t* with the Arabic article and the feminine ending, and it was the name in which Aksum entered Islamic history. Around 615 the Prophet sent a party of his followers across the sea to the Najāshī, the negus, of al-Ḥabasha, a just king in whose land, he told them, no one was wronged; the Meccans remembered the Abyssinian army of Abraha that had marched on their city with an elephant in the year of the Prophet's birth, the subject of a sūra of the Qurʾān. The geographers kept the name for the highland kingdom and its Christian successors, and the collective *ḥabash* for its people, and the word is the Arabic name of Ethiopia still.

The entry is the name that reached Europe. Where the Greeks and their heirs said Ethiopia and the Syriac churches said Kush, Arabic alone of the great traditions kept the Arabian neighbors' name for the highlanders, and the Arabic form, carried by the Portuguese and then by every European language, became Abyssinia, the name of the country in Western usage into the twentieth century. The oldest exonym on the page, first carved in Sabaic around 200, is thus the one with the longest run: two thousand years from the inscriptions of Marib to the newspapers of the twentieth century, with Arabic as the carrier for most of them.
