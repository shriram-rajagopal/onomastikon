*Armenia* is the Latin name, taken from the Greek *Armenía* and used throughout Roman geography, history, and statecraft. Armenia was a perennial concern of Rome, the contested buffer kingdom between the empire and Parthia, and Tacitus famously summed up its position as a people "ambiguous" by temperament and situation, set between two great powers. The name fills the Roman record of the eastern wars, from Pompey and Mark Antony to the long Julio-Claudian struggle over who would crown the Armenian king.

Through Latin the name reached its permanent form, the *Armenia* shared without change by the modern European languages. The route is the familiar one: an Iranian name first cut at Behistun, rendered into Greek, and fixed in Latin without further change. The endonym *Haykʿ* remained sealed within the Armenian world throughout, so that the country entered Western consciousness, as it has stayed, under the name its first imperial masters gave it.
