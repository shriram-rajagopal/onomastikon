*Māday* is the name of the Medes in Imperial Aramaic, the administrative lingua franca of the Achaemenid Empire, written in the consonantal Aramaic alphabet without vowel signs. It appears in the documentary Aramaic of the empire's chancelleries, including the papyri and ostraca of the Jewish garrison at Elephantine in Egypt, where the calendar and the dating formulae move in the orbit of Persian and Median officials, and the Median month-names and titles surface in everyday correspondence.

Aramaic was the connective tissue of the empire, the script in which a Persian decree issued at Susa could be read from Egypt to Bactria, and its form of the name is the workaday counterpart to the monumental Old Persian. The unpointed *mdy* is also the immediate kin of the Hebrew *Māday*, the two Northwest Semitic forms differing only in the vowels the Aramaic leaves unwritten; through Aramaic, the Iranian name circulated in the same administrative world that the Hebrew Bible later drew on for its account of the Medes.
