The Babylonian Aramaic name for the imperial city, *Rōmī*, the Greek *Rhṓmē* in Aramaic dress, the term by which the Talmud names the power that destroyed the Temple and ruled the western world. Beside the plain *Rōmī* the Bavli also names Rome under the cipher *Edom*, the hostile brother-nation of Jacob, a coded usage that let the rabbis write of the empire and its sins without naming it outright.

*Rōmī* is striking for where it is spoken from. The Talmud was made in Sasanian Babylonia, under Persian rule, yet Rome looms over its imagination as the great destroyer and the eschatological foe, the city of the burning of the Temple and of the wicked kingdom whose fall the rabbis awaited. It is the rare name on this page given by people who never lived under the power they named: the Jews of the Persian East, naming the western empire they remembered as the author of their exile.
