*Mōuru* (𐬨𐬋𐬎𐬭𐬎) is the Avestan name of the Merv oasis, listed in the first chapter of the *Vidēvdād* among the sixteen good lands created by Ahura Mazdā, each with the counter-creation the evil spirit inflicted on it; Mōuru appears near the head of the list, in the company of Sogdiana and Bactria, described as strong and truthful. The catalogue is the sacred geography of the eastern Iranian world, and its date is the old problem of everything Avestan; the entry carries the conventional window for orally composed material fixed long before writing.

Its value on the page is what it does to the Behistun entry beside it. *Marguš* shows the oasis as an empire's possession; *Mōuru* shows the same place, in the same period or earlier, as a unit of religious cosmology, one of the lands that exist because God made them individually. The two attestations are independent, cognate reflexes of one name in the two oldest Iranian traditions, scripture and chancery, and neither knows the other exists. Merv is among the handful of places on this atlas that enter the record simultaneously as a province and as a creation, and the name was the same in both ledgers; the tax roll and the scripture agree on nothing about the place except what to call it.
