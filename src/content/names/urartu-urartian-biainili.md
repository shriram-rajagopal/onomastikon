*Biainili* is the kingdom's name for itself, attested in its own royal inscriptions in the Urartian language and the borrowed Neo-Assyrian cuneiform in which they were carved. It is a plural or collective formation, "the lands of Biaina," Biaina being the core territory around Lake Van; the kings style themselves rulers "of the Bia lands," much as a Hittite or Assyrian king ruled a cluster of territories under one crown. The lake itself, and the modern city of Van on its eastern shore, still carry an echo of the old central name.

This is the rarest kind of name to recover: the genuine endonym of a kingdom known to everyone else by an enemy's word. Where *Urarṭu* fills the Assyrian annals and reaches the Bible as *Ararat*, *Biainili* stays inside the kingdom's own monuments and effectively died with it, leaving almost no trace in the wider record. The Armenians who later took the highland were a different people with a different language and gave it yet another name again. Few states have left so complete a gap between what they were called and what they called themselves, and fewer still have had their self-name survive only in the lake at their heart.
