*Iconium* is the Latin form of the Greek name, in the record from Cicero, who as governor of Cilicia in 51 BCE marched his army through the city and dated dispatches from it, Iconium serving as a mustering point for his province's defense. Under the empire it became a Roman colony, Colonia Aelia Hadriana Iconiensium, without ever losing its plain name; the Vulgate's rendering of Acts carried *Iconium* into the Latin Bible, which made the city's name familiar across medieval Europe in churches that could not have placed it on a map.

The Latin entry's work on this page is transmission to the west. It was through the Vulgate's *Iconium*, read aloud wherever Acts was read, that the city stayed in western memory across the centuries when no Latin traveler saw it, so that when crusaders reached the plateau in 1097 and 1190 they knew they were fighting at a city of Saint Paul; the chronicles of Barbarossa's last campaign, which stormed the town, write *Iconium* with the Bible's spelling. A provincial governor's dispatches and a missionary's rough reception preserved the name in Latin; the second proved the stronger vehicle, and for the Latin west the sultans of Rūm reigned, to the end, in a verse of scripture.
