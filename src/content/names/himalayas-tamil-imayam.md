The Tamil name of the Himalaya, *Imayam*, the Sanskrit snow-mountain name with its initial *h-* dropped at the door, as Tamil regularly drops it, and the ending reshaped to the Tamil noun class in *-am*; whether the immediate model was *Himavat* or a form of *Himālaya* the Tamil shape does not say, and the atlas leaves the vector open. In the Sangam panegyrics the mountain is the fixed northern limit of the world: a king's fame runs "from Kumari to the Imayam," from the cape at Tamil country's southern tip to the snows, and the Chera kings claimed the mountain in their very titles as *Imayavarampaṉ*, "he whose boundary is the Himalaya." The Cilappatikāram makes the claim literal, quarrying the heroine's image-stone there.

The page's other names look up at the range from many directions, but the Tamil one is measured from the greatest distance: a people of the tropical far south, most of whom would never see snow at all, kept the snow mountain as one pole of a formula whose other pole was their own southern cape. The mountain and the cape between them defined the world an ambitious Tamil king was supposed to fill. Greek and Latin set the range at the edge of their maps; the Tamil poets set it at the edge of ambition, and a Chera dynasty wore the boundary as a personal name.
