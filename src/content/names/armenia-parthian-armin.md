The Parthian form of Armenia's name, from the Parthian version of Šābuhr I's trilingual inscription at the Kaʿba-ye Zardušt, c. 262 CE, written *ʾrmny* where the Middle Persian column writes *ʾlmny*. The early Sasanian kings published their res gestae in Middle Persian, Parthian, and Greek, the three chancellery languages of the empire they had taken from the Arsacids, and Armenia appears in all three columns of the province list.

The trilingual habit is itself the observation. This page already holds Armenia in the three parallel scripts of the Achaemenid chancellery, Old Persian, Elamite, and Akkadian; the ŠKZ shows the successor empire doing exactly the same thing with a new set of three, and Armenia sitting in both trilinguals a full millennium apart. Parthian was months from extinction as a chancellery language when this was carved, kept on the wall mostly to honor the dynasty the Sasanians had displaced. The name is ordinary; the column it stands in is a monument to the last generation in which anyone would write it.
