*Májiā* (麻嘉) is the Song Chinese name for Mecca, recorded in Zhao Rukuo's *Zhufanzhi* of about 1225 among the lands of the *Dàshí*. The characters transcribe the Arabic name as the sea merchants pronounced it, and the entry's information is recognizably the hajj at one remove: Zhao reports that the peoples of the *Dàshí* lands journey there year after year from every quarter, and describes the great cubical shrine at the center of the city. What he cannot do is classify it, and so the *Zhufanzhi* presents the founder of the faith as a Buddha and the Kaaba as the house where he lived, Islam filed under the one category of foreign holiness that Song China possessed.

The entry is therefore a double transmission, and only half of it worked. The name crossed perfectly: *Májiā* is Makka, sound for sound, as exact as maritime hearsay could make it. The religion crossed as translation into the nearest available frame, pilgrimage read as Buddhist pilgrimage, prophet read as Buddha, sanctuary read as relic-house. On this atlas names regularly outtravel understanding, but rarely so visibly side by side in a single paragraph of one gazetteer. The sounds of a name survive any journey intact; what the name means has to be rebuilt at every border from local materials, and in Quanzhou the local material was Buddhism.
