The Persian name of Egypt, *Miṣr*, taken whole from Arabic and carried in Persian literature from the Shāhnāma onward; the classical pronunciation moves the vowel toward *Mesr*, but the word is the Arabic one, emphatic ṣ and all, and Persian has used it ever since. It displaced the older Iranian tradition the atlas records on this page: the Achaemenid chancellery had called Egypt *Mudrāya* and the Sasanian books *Misr* already in a late borrowed form, so New Persian was the second Iranian language in a row to take Egypt's name from a Semitic neighbor.

*Miṣr* makes Persian the twelfth language of the *mṣr* family, and its interest is what the borrowing replaced. Old Persian *Mudrāya* was itself a loan from the same Semitic root, taken in the sixth century BCE; New Persian *Miṣr* is the same root borrowed again fifteen hundred years later from a different Semitic empire. Iranian never developed its own name for Egypt at all: twice conquering or facing the country, twice it adopted the name its Semitic-speaking neighbors used, so that the page's oldest exonym family claims Persian at both ends of Iranian history. Some names are transmitted; this one was simply re-delivered, by a new postman, to the same address.
