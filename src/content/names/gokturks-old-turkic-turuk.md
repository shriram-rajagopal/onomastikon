The endonym, *Türük*, written 𐱅𐰇𐰼𐰜 in the runiform script of the Orkhon inscriptions, where the shorter spelling *Türk* also occurs. The stelae of Kül Tegin and Bilge Qaghan use the name relentlessly, above all in the formula *Türük bodun*, "the Türk people," whom the inscriptions address, scold, mourn, and command; the name is at once the state's, the people's, and, in the qaghans' rhetoric, a charge laid on both by heaven. Its further etymology is uncertain, and the tradition that reads it as "strong" is not securely older than the name itself.

This is the youngest endonym in the atlas to found a world-family, and it did so faster than any other. Within a single generation of the qaghanate's rise the name stands in Chinese annals, and within two in Byzantine history; the page around this entry is the record of its spread. What the inscriptions themselves add is the interior view every borrowed form lacks: in other languages *Turk* names a power on the horizon, while on the Orkhon stones *Türük* names a fragile people whose kings plead with it, in writing, not to scatter. The world's word for an empire began as an empire's word for the people it feared to lose.
