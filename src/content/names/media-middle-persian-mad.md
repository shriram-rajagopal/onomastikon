The Middle Persian name of Media, attested as *Mād* in the province list of Šābuhr I's trilingual inscription of c. 262 CE, standing among the lands of Ērānšahr between the Caucasian provinces and Gurgān. The form continues Old Persian *Māda* with the final vowel worn away; in the later Pahlavi centuries the final consonant lenited in speech, giving the shape *Māh* that the early Islamic administration then borrowed for its Median tax districts.

The entry supplies the missing link in this page's longest chain. Between Darius's *Māda* and the Arabic *Māh* of the caliphate's ledgers lie eleven hundred years, and this form is the bridge: the Sasanian bureaucracy carried the Achaemenid name forward as a working provincial label for a people who no longer existed, and passed it, already worn down by centuries of speech, into Arabic. Every stage of the name survives in an administrative document and not one in a Median voice. Media is the atlas's clearest case of a name kept alive entirely by other people's paperwork.
