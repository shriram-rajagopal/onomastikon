The Armenian name of India, *Hndikkʿ*, the plural of *hndik*, "Indian," on the model that names countries by their peoples throughout the Armenian column of this atlas, *Haykʿ*, *Yoynkʿ*, *Parskʿ*. The word is an Iranian loan, the Middle Persian *Hind* of India's own page fitted with an Armenian suffix, and it carries the *h*- that Iranian put on the old river-name *Sindhu*; the *Ašxarhacʿoycʿ* describes India among the countries of Asia, and the form persists into modern Armenian.

*Hndikkʿ* is the *sindhu* family's Armenian station, and it shows the family's great sound-change riding one language further than the empire that made it. Armenian never bordered India and never heard *Sindhu* from an Indian; it heard *Hind* from the Persians, as the Greeks had heard *Hinduš* before dropping the *h* the other way. Every westward path of India's name passes through Iranian, and Armenian, embedded in the Iranian world, took the name at its source. A country of the far southeast thus enters Armenian in exactly the same dress as Rome from the far southwest: whatever direction a name came from, for Armenia it arrived speaking Persian.
