*Kušaya* is Kush in the Elamite versions of the Achaemenid lists, written *ku-šá-a-ia* behind the AŠ sign that Elamite set before place-names, the determinative the atlas keeps because the stone keeps it. The form is the Old Persian name in Elamite transcription, taken over in its ethnic shape, *Kūšiyā*, the Kushites, rather than the bare name of the land, and it stands in the Elamite column of the trilingual lists exactly where the Old Persian stands in its own. Elamite was the middle language of the Achaemenid chancery, the one in which the Persepolis administration kept its accounts, and it rendered the foreign names of the empire's edges by ear from the Persian.

The entry is the middle pillar of this page's trilingual, as the Elamite *Mudraya* is on Egypt's, and it illustrates the limits of the arrangement. Elamite had no word of its own for any land in Africa; the name it writes is Persian in Elamite syllables, which is to say the chancery's word, not the scribe's. What Elamite adds is confirmation: that the Persian name was pronounced with the long *u* the Semitic forms share, and that in the imperial lists Kush was a people before it was a place, a column of tribute-bearers rather than a province with a governor.
