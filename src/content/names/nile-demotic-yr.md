The Demotic name of the Nile, *yr*, "the river": the worn-down descendant of the Middle Egyptian *jtrw*, the ordinary word for river that had always, in Egypt, meant one river above all. The contracts, letters, and tales of the Demotic corpus cross, tax, and travel the *yr* as a matter of course; Egypt at no stage of its language needed a proper name for the Nile, and the definite article did the work a name does elsewhere. The lowercase transliteration is deliberate, for unlike this page's *Ḥꜥpy*, the inundation personified and divine, *yr* is not a name at all but the common noun in its capacity as one.

The entry closes the middle of the *jtrw* family's arc, and the family is a strange one: its membership runs from Hebrew *Yeʾōr*, the Egyptian word borrowed into the Bible where pharaoh's river needed naming, to Coptic *Eioor*, the same word's final Egyptian form, with the Demotic *yr* now standing between them. Egyptian is thus the only language in the atlas whose word for its river was adopted by a foreign scripture as a proper name while remaining, at home, no name at all. Outsiders heard "the river" and took it for the river's name; Egypt just kept saying the river, for three thousand years, because when there is only one, that is its name.
