The Middle Persian name of the river, *Frāt*, attested in the *Bundahišn*, whose chapter on the rivers inventories the waters of the world and gives the Frāt its rising on the Roman frontier and its flowing to join the Diglat, noting that its water is drawn off for irrigation across the land. Whether the form continues the Achaemenid *Ufrātu* with its initial vowel worn away or was taken fresh from the Aramaic of Sasanian Mesopotamia, the result is the same plain Semitic shape that Syriac wrote as *Pǝrāṯ* and that Arabic would carry on as *al-Furāt*. The attestation is Book Pahlavi, which has no Unicode encoding, so the entry carries transliteration only.

The form is the undressing of a name. The Old Persian entry on this page shows the Achaemenid chancellery wearing the river's Semitic name with an Iranian costume over it, an *Ufrātu* that Persian ears could hear as "good to cross"; a thousand years later the Persian of the Zoroastrian books says *Frāt*, the folk-etymology gone, the name back to the bare shape its Mesopotamian neighbors had used all along. Empires decorate the names of the things they rule, and the decorations do not outlast them; the river that outlived both empires kept the undecorated word.
